### HASK'S 19 gp IS NOT SPREAD ACROSS THE 693 — it is ALL IN THE SHEARS

**Asked which of the freight his share was buying, he chose one line and only
one** *(d6 = 4, day 28, on the track back from Ede's)*.

| holder | in | against | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HASK** | **19 gp** | **the 136 gp Fenn tool list** — 30 pair of shears, scythe blades, sickles, files, an auger | **19 ÷ 136 = 13.97%, near enough ONE PART IN SEVEN.** About **four pair of shears and a bit** |
| The party | 674 | everything, the Fenn line included | the rest |

**He is NOT a flat 3% of the cargo.** That was the first offer and he refused the
shape of it. **His money is concentrated in one line**, which means his risk and
his return are concentrated there too — and the tool line is the highest-turning
thing on the carts, so this is better for him than spreading, not worse. **He did
not choose it for that reason and Wend told him so to his face**, precisely so he
could not think he had been handed a kindness.

**His reason, and he never quite says it:** *"I've stood in a lot of yards at
night. You get to know a holding by the look of it — what's kept up, what's let
go. A man making do with a worn pair of shears has a particular kind of gate. I'd
sooner my money went to fixing that than to spices."*

**The at-cost iron does not touch him** — his stake is nowhere near that line.

### How the asking went — d100 = 24, and it landed badly

Being asked to *pick*, in front of Wend who prices everything and Fenn who
priced the tools, read to him as a test he could only fail. His first answer was
flat and defensive — *"Same as yours. Three in the hundred of the lot. That's the
answer that doesn't make me a fool"* — and he rode two hours ahead before coming
back with the real one.

**Wend's read, and it is the note to keep:** *"He's not wrong that it was a test.
He's wrong about who was being tested."*

**Lesson for handling him: he will not choose in an audience.** Give him the
question and then give him the road.

### What this actually is

**Hask declined coin and took stock.** He is not on wages and he is not on a
share of somebody else's venture — **he is a part-owner of specific goods**, at
one part in seven of the tool line, and his money is at exactly the same risk as
the party's for exactly the same reward.

**It should be written down like everything else here**, and Wend writes it,
because the whole reason this venture is on paper is Rell's brother and a promise
made out loud in a yard. **Hask of all people knows what an unwritten promise is
worth**; he spent twenty-two years on the other side of one.

> **RESOLVED by his own choice.** The at-cost iron wrinkle no longer applies to
> him: he is in the tool line, not spread across the pot, so nothing of his is
> earmarked to return cost-only. **The wrinkle still exists for the party's own
> 674** — 100 of it makes nothing by design.

**The strategic purchase.** Wend had this list in her head for years.

| | cost |
|---|---|
| **Broad loom** — the real thing, **dismantled for the road**. Tristle took it apart; Bern rebuilds it | **60 gp** |
| Distaffs and spindles ×20 | 2 gp |
| Wool cards, 6 pairs | 12 gp |
| **Flax hackles ×2** — for Fenn's crop specifically | 6 gp |
| Dye — woad and madder | 20 gp |

**None of this is priced in the PHB or DMG. Every figure is a DM call**, made in
play and recorded here so it stays consistent.

**Wend refused the spinning wheels** (8 gp each): *"For a hundred gold of wheels
I'd have twelve spinners. For two gold of spindles I'd have twenty — and twenty
people spinning badly becomes twenty people spinning well, and that's the whole
trick."*

### 2 · Bern's tools — 25 gp — **ON THE CARTS, but not stock**

Try-plane, a set of good chisels, augers, a fine-toothed saw. **A genuine upgrade
on the 30 gp kit bought him blind in Wethersby** — a staple town has better steel
by a wide margin. **A gift. Not in the 693 and not for sale.**

### 3 · Banded mail ×2 — 180 gp — **WORN. NOT FREIGHT, NOT STOCK**

Hask and Dellie, *(d6 = 3, both gladly)*. **AC 8 → AC 4**, and **AC 3** with the
two large shields they have since been given. Full sheets in `campaign/npcs.md`.

### 4 · Fenn's tool list — 136 gp — **ON THE CARTS**

Fenn prices tools for a living and was standing right there. He did not hesitate:

> **"Shears."** *"You're a wool district… a bad pair of shears tears the fleece

> steel's what a small town can't make well and won't stock, because they only
> sell once a year and a smith would rather do horseshoes. Buy thirty pair.
> They'll go before you've got the cart unloaded."*

### 5 · Iron, nails and salt — 100 gp — **ON THE CARTS. 600 lb**

The heaviest thing on the load and about half its weight.

**100 gp is what was PAID.** Selling it to the district **at cost** is the
*plan*, not the purchase — Wend's "open-handed where it is FINITE and where it
SHOWS" rule from `campaign/wool_venture.md`. A kindness of a known amount that
does not compound into an obligation.

**So it sits in the 693 as stock at cost, and is expected to return its cost and
no margin.** If that plan changes, it changes for Hask too — say so.

### 6 · Fenn's barn, seed and winter — 90 gp — **PAID OUT DAY 27. NOT FREIGHT**

He worked it out on his fingers, which is how you know it is honest.

| | |
|---|---|
| The barn — timber, thatch, ironwork; **his two sons do the labour** | ~65 gp |
| Seed for four acres of flax | ~10 gp |
| Keeping three men to Michaelmas | ~15 gp |

*"I'd not take a penny more than that, because I'd have to pay it back out of
flax and I'd rather owe you ninety than a hundred and fifty."*

**Wend put in 5 gp of her own alongside**, on the same buy-back terms, and takes
her share **in flax not coin**. The crop share therefore divides about
**eighteen to one** in the party's favour; she raised that herself and said she
did not care — *"I want the fibre, not the fraction."*

**This is a separate venture from the goods.** Hask's three in a hundred is in
the *cargo*, not in Fenn's flax.
