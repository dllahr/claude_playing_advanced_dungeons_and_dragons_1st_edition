# The Articles — what is written down, and why everything gets written down

**Night of day 32, Wethersby.** Three separate people asked to be put in writing
in the space of one evening, and **all three named the same dead arrangement:**
Rell's brother, eleven years, a promise made out loud in a yard, nothing on paper,
nothing owed.

Wend writes them. Ottir witnesses. **A copy stays in Wethersby whenever the party
is away**, which is the entire point.

This file is the register. `campaign/wool_venture.md` holds the venture's
commercial terms; this holds the standing arrangements about **people and
authority**.

---

## 1 · SUCCESSION — Wend's price for taking the factorship

**She asked for this and it is not about money.** Her argument, and it is
unanswerable:

> *"Torsat's got six hit points. Frython's got **two**. I've watched you carry a
> man in on a door. So: what happens to this when one of you doesn't come back
> up? **Not if.** You've all said it yourselves — it's not if, it's when."*
>
> *"There's eleven people on a wage in that barn. There's six holders about to
> sign a thing with your name on it. Who holds Dave's share? Who pays the eleven
> that month? Who tells Marrow? Every partnership I've ever seen written was
> written by somebody who thought about this in the good week rather than the
> bad one."*

### THE TERMS AS GIVEN

| | |
|---|---|
| **If the party does not come back** | **WEND MANAGES THE MONEY.** All of it — the party's capital, its shares, its interest in the goods |
| **For how long** | **TWO YEARS** |
| **If there is still no word at the end of two years** | **Wend begins spending it on the town, as she sees fit.** No further instruction, no conditions, no reversion. **Her judgement** |

**That is the whole of it and it is deliberately short.** No trustee, no
committee, no schedule of purposes. The party has handed a small cloth trader
the disposal of everything it owns on a two-year timer and told her to use her
own head.

**Ottir witnesses. A copy stays in Wethersby.**

> **DM note — do not soften this if it fires.** Six 1st-level characters with 2-9
> hit points are going back into Quasqueton. If they die there, this document is
> the campaign's ending and Wend is the one who spends it. It should be played
> straight and in full.

## 2 · COLL'S AUTHORITY — what he may do while the party is away

**He asked for this and his reason is the best one in the file:**

> *"Not because I don't trust you. Because you're going to be four hundred foot
> under a hill and I'm going to be stood in a barn with somebody's mother in
> front of me, and **I'll want to know the answer before she asks it, not after.**"*

### WHAT HE HAS ALREADY BEEN TOLD — the standing instruction, day 12

Recorded here verbatim because it is load-bearing and was once misremembered:

> *"if there's anyone desperate around, who's out on the road — **I need you bring
> them in**, get them working with you all on the wool, or on the town, or on our
> basecamp."*
>
> *"Thank you Coll, you've got a good plan, **if people need work I trust your
> judgement**."*

**He did exactly that.** Four regulars and two occasional hands walked into the
barn while the party was at Ludbury; he worked them, fed them from the store, and
**wrote down what each was owed**, because the party had taken every coin south.
He told each of them to their face that the debt was real and that they could
stand in the square and say so if it went unpaid.

**Paid in arrears day 32: 62 person-days at a labourer's shilling = 3 gp 2 sp.**

## 3 · ISTA — one line, in Wend's hand

Asked for on the road, day 31, after two months of asking to go underground and
never once being answered.

> *"**Put me on the list.** She's written down every man's wage and every holder's
> share and what Fenn's owed in flax and what Hask's nineteen gold bought. Put me
> on it. One line. Next time you go, I go — in her hand, where you'll trip over
> it."*

**Granted. Written. It contains the word "underground."**

### Her role — and it is a DUTY, not a courtesy

**The lantern and the fuel.** Dave's framing, and it is why she took it:

> *"That's serious business down there and it'll be one less thing for us to worry
> about when things come to blows. Because to be clear — it's not **if** we get in
> a fight down there, it's a matter of **when**."*

She has counted the stock by hand: **three lanterns** (two hooded, plus the dry
one out of the area 6 closet that fills and lights), **15 oil flasks, 8 torches,
6 candles.** She asked whether hooding stops the burn. **It does not** — the party
once slept a full night in area 5 and woke down eight hours of oil nobody had
counted. That is now somebody's job.

### Her gear — she asked for less than was offered

- **Leather, not mail.** *"I'd be no use and I'd be slow."*
- Something she can actually swing
- **A lantern of her own and oil for it** — she named this before the armour
- **A small set of joiner's tools.** *"You've a thief who can take a bed apart.
  There's four of you standing about while he does it. I can be a second pair of
  hands on a frame — and that's a real thing, not a made-up job."*

**Not yet bought. Comfortably inside current funds.**

### Bern

**She told him herself on the night of day 32 and asked that nobody help.**
He will not go near the caves and is ashamed of it. Outcome unknown — **do not
resolve it for her.**

## 4 · HASK — a seventh of the tool line

19 gp of his own, chosen into the shears rather than spread. Full working in
`campaign/cargo_manifest.md`. **Still only on the back of something in Wend's
hand; it belongs in the fair copy with the rest.**

**He has also asked to do the selling himself** — the first time he has ever asked
this party for a job. **Granted, with a condition Dave phrased carefully:**

> *"I'll not throw anyone into a deep pond before they've done some swimming where
> they can stand."*

**Wend coaches: three days.** She does four gates while he watches, he does four
while she watches, then he is on his own and she is two fields away. **She will
not correct him in front of a customer** — *"there's only six holdings in this
hundred."*

**Reaction 109, the highest in the campaign, and it was for being told he needed
coaching.**

## THE PATTERN — worth knowing before offering anybody anything

Four reactions in two days, all to acts of kindness:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| Hask, asked to *pick* his share in front of Wend | **24** | badly |
| Osk, apologised to in a full yard | **24** | badly |
| Ista, offered the loom | **9** | badly |
| Bern, given better tools | **8** | badly |
| **Ista, given the lantern as a DUTY** | **71** | well |
| **Bern, shown the accounts and offered ONGOING PAID WORK** | **68** | well |
| **Hask, handed a DECISION as a partner** | **100** | enthusiastically |
| **Hask, given a JOB with coaching** | **109** | enthusiastically |

**Gifts land badly. Work lands.** Every one of these people has been on the
receiving end of something and has nothing to give back; a further gift deepens a
debt they cannot pay, and a **job cancels it.**

Wend named half of it herself and got the room right and the cause wrong:

> *"You put a kindness on a man in front of an audience… You're not wrong about
> people. You're wrong about **rooms**."*

**It was never only the room.** Dave took Ista aside privately and still got a 9.
It was the **debt**. The fix is not privacy, it is **employment**.

## 5 · GYLL'S RESTITUTION — written by Ottir, day 35

**Eleven names. 4 gp 10 sp apiece. Fifty gold.** He had 38 and is selling the grey
mare for the rest.

**NINE PAID. TWO SET ASIDE** — **Ned Tapp** and the lad called **Wisp**, both gone
south, neither heard of. Coll's condition and the whole town heard it:

> *"If they never come, it sits there. **That's what owing is.**"*

**A copy stays in Wethersby.** If either man ever walks back into this hundred,
his money is waiting and has been waiting, and that fact is more valuable to this
district than the coin.

**The party did not contribute and should not.** Gyll never asked.

## 6 · HASK'S FIRST SOLO SALE — day 35, and he lost nine gold

**d100 = 33.** Wend two fields away as agreed, saying nothing.

Marrow took **all ten remaining pair of shears** *(d6 = 6 — he has half the
district's sheep and six new hands and shearing in May; the sale made itself)*.

**Wend's price: 3 gp 10 sp a pair = 35 gp. Hask took 26.** He named the price,
Marrow did not answer instantly, and **Hask filled the silence by lowering it.**

**A seventh of that discount was his own money.**

> **The lesson, and Wend gave it that night and not in the yard, as promised:**
> *"He'd have paid the full price. He needed them more than you needed to sell
> them. **You weren't selling. You were apologising.**"*

**This is the shape of him and it will recur:** a man who spent twenty-two years
taking things from people cannot ask people for money, so he **discounts as a way
of not-taking**. It is not lack of nerve — he faced Halm down in a thicket. It is
that a price feels like a demand.

**Do not fix this with a rule.** He gets better at it or he doesn't, and Wend has
another two days of coaching she is now going to spend entirely on this.
