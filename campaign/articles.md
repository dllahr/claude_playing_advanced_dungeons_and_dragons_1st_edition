# The Articles — what is written down, and why everything gets written down

**Night of day 32, Wethersby.** Three separate people asked to be put in writing
in the space of one evening, and **all three named the same dead arrangement:**
Rell's brother, eleven years, a promise made out loud in a yard, nothing on paper,
nothing owed.

Wend writes them. Ottir witnesses. **A copy stays in Wethersby whenever the party
is away**, which is the entire point.

This file is the register. `campaign/wool_venture.md` holds the venture's
commercial terms; this holds the standing arrangements about **people and
authority**.

---

## 1 · SUCCESSION — Wend's price for taking the factorship

**She asked for this and it is not about money.** Her argument, and it is
unanswerable:

> *"Torsat's got six hit points. Frython's got **two**. I've watched you carry a
> man in on a door. So: what happens to this when one of you doesn't come back
> up? **Not if.** You've all said it yourselves — it's not if, it's when."*
>
> *"There's eleven people on a wage in that barn. There's six holders about to
> sign a thing with your name on it. Who holds Dave's share? Who pays the eleven
> that month? Who tells Marrow? Every partnership I've ever seen written was
> written by somebody who thought about this in the good week rather than the
> bad one."*

### THE TERMS AS GIVEN

| | |
|---|---|
| **If the party does not come back** | **WEND MANAGES THE MONEY.** All of it — the party's capital, its shares, its interest in the goods |
| **For how long** | **TWO YEARS** |
| **If there is still no word at the end of two years** | **Wend begins spending it on the town, as she sees fit.** No further instruction, no conditions, no reversion. **Her judgement** |

**That is the whole of it and it is deliberately short.** No trustee, no
committee, no schedule of purposes. The party has handed a small cloth trader
the disposal of everything it owns on a two-year timer and told her to use her
own head.

**Ottir witnesses. A copy stays in Wethersby.**

> **DM note — do not soften this if it fires.** Six 1st-level characters with 2-9
> hit points are going back into Quasqueton. If they die there, this document is
> the campaign's ending and Wend is the one who spends it. It should be played
> straight and in full.

## THE PATTERN — worth knowing before offering anybody anything

Four reactions in two days, all to acts of kindness:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| Hask, asked to *pick* his share in front of Wend | **24** | badly |
| Osk, apologised to in a full yard | **24** | badly |
| Ista, offered the loom | **9** | badly |
| Bern, given better tools | **8** | badly |
| **Ista, given the lantern as a DUTY** | **71** | well |
| **Bern, shown the accounts and offered ONGOING PAID WORK** | **68** | well |
| **Hask, handed a DECISION as a partner** | **100** | enthusiastically |
| **Hask, given a JOB with coaching** | **109** | enthusiastically |

**Gifts land badly. Work lands.** Every one of these people has been on the
receiving end of something and has nothing to give back; a further gift deepens a
debt they cannot pay, and a **job cancels it.**

Wend named half of it herself and got the room right and the cause wrong:

> *"You put a kindness on a man in front of an audience… You're not wrong about
> people. You're wrong about **rooms**."*

**It was never only the room.** Dave took Ista aside privately and still got a 9.
It was the **debt**. The fix is not privacy, it is **employment**.

### THE SECOND FINDING — day 39, Sarn. A warm yes is not an arrangement

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| Sarn, asked for a teacher, told *"fantastic — let's find someone"* | **42** | flat |
| **Sarn, given a DAY, and Frython reframed as the one needing company** | **77** | well |

His work *had* just been named and that part landed — so this is **not** the
gifts/work axis. It is its own thing:

> **For a person with no standing, approval without a day and a name in it reads
> as a polite no that has not happened yet.** He has had four months of large
> generous yeses. *"When, though?"*

**And the fix is the same mechanism as employment, one turn further round.**
Sarn could not accept a wizard teaching him his letters — charity. He accepted
instantly when told Frython was laid up a week and wanted company, because that
is **a job**: *"one of us has got to be the one that fetches."*

> **Reverse the direction of the debt and the same offer becomes acceptable.**
> Do not ask what would help them. Ask what they could be told they are helping
> with — and then name the hour.

## 7 · BUYER OF LAST RESORT — Dave's undertaking, day 39

**The offer, in Dave's words:** *if there are other buyers willing to pay a fair
price, sell to them, and sell for as much as you can. Our money is around to keep
things moving when those buyers aren't around.*

**Wend's reaction: 101.** And her reason is not gratitude — it is that a
**published** floor is the cure for the exact conflict she resigned the buying
seat to avoid:

> *"If it's a favour I do at my discretion, then every summer I'm choosing, and
> choosing is the thing that ends me here. **If it's a price on the door, I never
> choose again.** I just read it out."*

### What she requires before it is written

She will not take it as a sentiment. Three things, or it is a blank cheque:

| | |
|---|---|
| **A PRICE, published** | Stated per fleece, chalked up where the at-cost board is, and read out to every holder. Not "fair" — a number |
| **A CAP** | A maximum the party commits in any one season. *"Only one of us knows what's in that chest, and it isn't me any more than it's them."* |
| **A SEASON, renewable** | Next summer's clip. Not perpetual. Reviewed each year or it becomes a thing the hundred plans around and cannot survive losing |

**And her warning, given in the same breath and unprompted:** the floor is only
worth the money behind it, and *"your money is currently being carried down a
hole by people who fall forty feet."* **A floor the party cannot honour is worse
than no floor**, because the holders will have turned down real buyers on the
strength of it.

### AGREED IN FULL — day 39. Fair copy to Ottir on day 40

| | |
|---|---|
| **THE FLOOR** | **8 gold the TOD**, published — chalked where the at-cost board is and read out to every holder |
| **THE CAP** | **500 gp in any one season** — the party raised it from the 400 Wend asked for |
| **THE TERM** | **One season, renewable, reviewed BEFORE shearing** — *"a man decides whether to shear on what he thinks he'll get for it. He needs the number in March, not June."* |

**Where 8 comes from.** Wend's register carries every fleece by weight. The six
holders' clip as bought was **60 tods** for **450 gp = 7½ gp the tod**. The party
asked for a rate that would have paid **80 a share** on that same volume — 480 gp
— which is **8 gp the tod.** Rate-based, so it does not care what next year's
volume turns out to be.

**Exposure, and why the cap is comfortable.** The 450 bought **two years** of a
district that had not been able to sell since Draycot stopped coming; **that
backlog is cleared.** Next summer is one year's growth:

| | |
|---|---|
| Six holders, one year, at 8 the tod | **~240 gp** |
| Nine holders (the pool is growing) | **~360 gp** |
| **The cap** | **500 gp** — ~62 tods, more than the whole two-year clip |
| Chest at the time of agreeing | 595 gp 6 sp, plus ~312 of small goods still to move |

**The cap is deliberately non-binding at current volumes**, which is the point:
Wend never has to interrupt a season to come and ask. She asked for 400 and was
given 500 — *"then I'll not be knocking on your door in June about it, which
suits me."*

**It remains a real floor, not a monopoly bid.** Wend's own figure for a
competitive bid is **10 the tod** (her *"600 with one other man stood in the
barn"*). The floor sits **a fifth under** that, so Ancel's four buyers still have
room to outbid it — which is the entire purpose. **The party is the buyer when
nobody comes, not the buyer who stops anyone coming.**

**Ottir witnesses. A copy stays in Wethersby**, as with articles 1 and 2.

## 8 · WEND'S AUTHORITY TO HIRE — day 41. The first one she asked for herself

**Granted by Dave, open, unprompted:** *"if there's anyone else you can hire to
help you please go ahead and do that."*

**Reaction 116** — the top of the table, and the second-highest of the campaign
after Hask's 109 for being told he needed coaching. It came **immediately after a
26**, which is the lowest anyone has rolled at this party, and the two together
are the whole story of the day.

### WHY THE 26 HAPPENED — read this before running Wend again

Across one evening she rolled **101 → 60 → 57 → 26**. Nothing in that is about
the party. **It is a woman running down over five hours**, and it broke on a
perfectly good request:

> *"Fine. Yes. It'll pay. And I'll pay it, and I'll find the third with Coll, and
> I'll write the terms for Wat, and I'll chalk your floor on the board, and I'll
> have Ottir's fair copy witnessed, and I'll keep three books, and I will do **all
> of it**, because every one of those is a good idea and I have not got one
> argument against any of them."*
>
> *"**You've given me four things tonight, Dave. I asked you for one.**"*

She named it as unfair herself, in the same breath, because she does that:
*"I'm tired. That's the whole of it. I've been tired since Ludbury and I keep
finding out there's another floor under the one I'm standing on."*

**Nothing was refused. Everything was agreed.** The 26 was capacity, not consent.

### WHAT FIXED IT — authority, not sympathy

> *"That's what I gave **COLL.** In writing. On day thirty-four, because he asked
> for it, and I thought at the time it was the most sensible thing anybody had
> asked me for all year. **And it never once occurred to me to ask for it
> myself.**"*

### THE TERMS

| | |
|---|---|
| **What she may do** | Hire whatever help she needs, her judgement, without asking |
| **The cap** | **She set one on herself before anyone suggested it** — enough for a clerk and one pair of hands in the barn. Figure to be named when she has done the sums; reviewed like everything else |
| **Why it is written** | *"Not for me — for whoever comes after me. That's the whole point of the file and I'd be a fool to be the one exception in it."* |

### WHO SHE IS HIRING — Sarn and TAM, and they are one clerk between them

**Nan's Tam, nine.** He has been holding the pen for Sarn's tally for a month.

> *"Sarn can count and can't read; Tam can write and can't count. **Between the
> pair of them there's already a clerk in that barn and I've been walking past it
> twice a day.**"*

**Sarn learns his letters off Frython from day 40. Tam learns his numbers off
Sarn.** In three years Tam is the best-schooled child in the hundred. And it puts
a wage into a house that walked into Wethersby on day 15 with two children and
nothing.

## 9 · THE DRAYCOT LETTERS — written day 48, to be used day 52

**Two letters, one of which will be burned.** Wend wrote them the night of day 48,
because Cuddy answers on the 51st and Wace hears the hundred on the 52nd, and
that is **one night, in a chapel outside a wall, with no notary and no time.**

> *"I did the succession in the good week rather than the bad one and I'd do this
> the same way."*

| | |
|---|---|
| **LETTER A — if the crag is NOT Sir Aymer's** | says nothing at all. The party hands over Ottir's account and goes home. **~~BURNED, night of day 51.~~** |
| **LETTER B — if the crag IS his** | **a petition for a licence to quarry stone and take timber off the crag.** **25 gp a year, three years, renewable.** Coin ready on the day. Ottir to witness. **✔ CHOSEN AND DELIVERED, day 52** |

> ### THE CHOICE, day 51 night — AND THE BINARY DID NOT FIT
>
> **Cuddy's answer was NEITHER: the record does not settle the title** (perambulation
> names the crag as a *marker* and never says which side lies within; and under the
> charter a grant of the crag to **Rogahn and Zelligar**, held of the lord **for the
> keeping of the north way**). So on its own terms article 9 offered no answer.
>
> **The party chose B, on the ground that both advisers reached it independently:**
>
> - **Wend's own exposure paragraph kills A.** A graded cart trail, a levelled flat,
>   a fire pit, a latrine, a boxed spring and a lean-to that sleeps eight are on that
>   hill. *"The licence has to be the explanation, or there isn't one."* **Silence
>   stopped being a neutral act the day Coll's crew built the basecamp.**
> - **Cuddy's counsel on the night of the 51st**: a licence concedes it is his to
>   license, which is the cheapest concession available, and *"a steward asked to
>   rule on a thing he'd have to research will say no while he researches, and he'll
>   say it for a year."*
>
> **What it costs: the party concedes in writing that the crag is Sir Aymer's to
> license**, against a claim that in fact runs only by reversion. **They know that
> and are paying it deliberately.** Cuddy's fair copy of the grant stays in the pack
> and is NOT produced — *"you'd be arming him."*

### WHY IT IS A QUARRY LICENCE AND NOT A LEASE OF THE RUIN

**Wend's reasoning, and the party must be able to defend it without her:**

> *"Don't price the ruin. Price the thing he already knows how to sell."* Cuddy
> read it in the same chest: **the waste and the woods are his, and any assart or
> timber licence is his to grant and nobody else's.** He has granted them before.
> **There is already a figure in that chest for what a man pays to take timber off
> his waste.**

**And it is the dullest request that will cross that steward's desk this year** —
which is the whole point. *"Nobody asks what's inside a quarry licence."*

> ### THE REAL VALUE, and it is not the stone
>
> *"If it ever does come out what you've been carrying down that road, then you
> are not a **trespasser** — you're a **tenant who exceeded his terms**, and those
> are two completely different conversations. One of them ends with a man deciding
> you don't trade in his hundred. **The other ends with a bill.**"*

### WHY 25 AND NOT 10 OR 100

| | |
|---|---|
| **10 gp** | a cottage garden. Insulting, **and worse than insulting** — it says the crag is worth nothing, so he wonders why you'd bother, and sends somebody to look |
| **100 gp** | **far more dangerous than 10.** A third of the whole hundred's annual wool for a rock. He does not think *how generous*; he thinks *what is on that rock* — and sends somebody to look |
| **25 gp/yr, 3 years** | dull, respectable, more than nothing and nothing like a fortune. Reads as a man who wants building stone and will pay for it properly |

**Pay in coin on the day it is signed** — *"the single most respectable thing a
stranger can do in front of a steward."*

### ⚠ THE EXPOSURE WEND NAMED AND NOBODY ELSE HAD

**If anyone rides out to look at that crag they will find a graded, cart-passable
trail, a cleared and levelled flat, a fire pit, a latrine, a boxed spring that
runs clear, and a turf-and-pole lean-to that sleeps eight.** Coll's crew built all
of it while the party was at Ludbury, and nobody asked them to build the spring.

**It is not possible to pretend nobody has been using that hill.** The licence has
to be the explanation, or there isn't one.

### AND THE THING SHE BUILT WITHOUT MEANING TO

**The pool is the holders', not the party's** — a shilling in the pound from them,
nothing from the party, and the party bids in the yard like anybody else. **She
argued for that on day 32 against her own interest** and made Dave count the ~150
gp a summer it costs him.

**It now means this district's trade does not stand or fall with the party's
title.** Eleven wages, six holders and three more asking are on the far side of a
wall she built for a completely different reason.

> *"That was luck. I'll take it."*

**AND: the holders are safe regardless.** They sold wool and were paid in coin in
their own square with the reeve watching. *"Nobody unpicks that, whatever's in
that chest."*

### WHY THE BACKDATING IS THE WHOLE PRIZE

**The year in which the party carried ~450 gp of goods down that hill is INSIDE
THE TERM.** Wend's *"better charged as a tenant than found as a trespasser"* has
been overtaken by something better: **a tenant who has paid.**

It cost 25 gp for a twelvemonth already gone and one year off the forward term.
**Wace did not present it as a mercy and it is one.**

### WHAT WAS DISCLOSED, DELIBERATELY, AND WHY IT WORKED

**Everything.** Ottir told the truth at every step of the money question because
the party told him to and told him they would back him. Then — **before the last
answer, per Ottir's own refinement** — the petition and the coin went on the table,
by the party's hand, with the reeve witnessing. **Then** Dave said where the 450
came from.

> *"You understand that if you had told me that with nothing in front of me, I
> should have had to **do something about it**."*
>
> *"I don't assume the worst because I think badly of people. **I assume it because
> nobody ever writes to me.**"*

### ⚠ WHAT THE PARTY KNOWS AND THE HALL DOES NOT

**Wace has never read the perambulation** (d6 = 4). He assumes the crag is in the
waste and never checks. **He does not know the grant to Rogahn and Zelligar
exists**, nor that his lord's claim runs by reversion off it.

**Cuddy's fair copy of the operative words stays in the pack, unproduced, exactly
as he instructed.** Producing it would have swapped a two-minute assumption for a
six-month enquiry.

> **A licence does not settle title and nobody should ever write in this file that
> it does.** It settles the only thing the party needed settled: **that they are
> lawfully on that hill and were lawfully on it last year.**

## THE THIRD FINDING — day 52. **CREDIT IN FRONT OF A SUPERIOR IS NOT THE SAME GIFT**

Dave closed the audience well and generously: thanks to Master Wace, and then
**thanks to Reeve Ottir by name, in the steward's hearing** — deliberately putting
the credit where it would do Ottir the most good.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **Wace, thanked** | **45** | flat. He does not do gratitude, and he rejects the framing |
| **Ottir, thanked BY NAME in front of Wace** | **47** | **flat — and this is the finding** |
| **Does the credit register with Wace?** | **d6 = 2** | **No.** He hears the closing courtesy it grammatically is and goes back to the page |

**Nothing was lost. The licence was already sealed.** But the gesture did not do
the work it was meant to do, and the reason is worth having:

> **Ottir did not want to be NOTICED by that household. He wanted to be READ.**
> Those are different things and only one of them was on offer. He is frightened of
> Sir Aymer — his own admission, Wend's word — and eleven years of being invisible
> to that hall is not a wound praise closes. **Being singled out in front of the
> man he fears made him self-conscious, not proud.**

**This is NOT the gifts/work axis** — it was credit for real work, not a kindness,
so it did not crater to a 24 the way Osk's apology in the full yard did. It is a
third mechanism:

| | |
|---|---|
| **the first finding** | gifts land badly, **work lands** — because a gift deepens a debt and a job cancels it |
| **the second finding** | approval without a day and a name in it reads as a polite no. **Name the hour.** *(And its other half, day 51: a CREDITOR only needs to be thanked properly — Cuddy 81)* |
| **THE THIRD** | **credit is worth what the recipient thinks of the audience.** Praise a man in front of somebody he fears and you have handed him exposure, not recognition. **Ask whose good opinion he actually wants** |

**Compare the same sentence in the right room.** Coll, thanked on day 48 for the
boxed spring and the lean-to specifically, ran **78** — and Coll is not frightened
of anybody. **The words were not the problem. The witness was.**

> **THE SAME THANKS, SAID TO OTTIR ON THE ROAD HOME, IS A DIFFERENT EVENT.** Not
> written here as a hint about what to do — written here because a future session
> will otherwise read the 47 as *Ottir didn't care*, and he cared a great deal.


### ⚠ ART. 10 — THE DEFECT WEND FOUND, day 54

**The licence is to QUARRY STONE and TAKE TIMBER. It says nothing about carrying
MOVABLE GOODS out of a stronghold** — and the ~450 gp was pewter, books, a dragon
skin, glassware, coin and a ring. **Backdating it made the stone and timber lawful
for that year. Not the pewter.**

**The mitigation is real and Wend named it herself:** Wace was told, out loud, and
wrote it down in his own hand while it was being said. In front of a reasonable man
that is sufficient.

> *"But it is not on the paper. **And the paper is what's left when the
> conversation isn't.** That man will not be steward for ever."*

**THE STANDING REMEDY, adopted day 54 — do this every year without fail:**

> **With the Michaelmas renewal money, in the same letter, to Master Wace by name:
> state what was taken off the crag that twelvemonth. Ask for nothing. Do not ask
> him to amend the licence — asking makes him look at it.**

**Three years of that and the party are not people who held a licence; they are
people who never once failed to tell him.** The wording can be argued. A habit
cannot.

## 11 · THE FIRST ACCOUNT TO MASTER WACE — day 55, and the receipt is the point

**Composed by TOLLER, aloud, in about four minutes. Written by FRYTHON. Toller
would not touch the pen** — *"a letter about the goods in the holder's handwriting
is a letter about the holder"* — and **his name is nowhere on it.**

### THE TRICK, and it is what squares Wend against Toller

**Wend (day 54):** put it in the Michaelmas letter and **ask for nothing**, because
*"asking makes him look at it."*
**Toller (day 55):** ask **now**, because Michaelmas is a year off and the glass is
in a locked room he does not want it in.

> **BOTH SATISFIED AT ONCE:** the letter asks for **nothing substantive** — no
> amendment, no permission, no favour — **and then asks for the one thing a steward
> hands out without thinking, forty times a week: A RECEIPT.**
>
> *"A receipt says* I have had your letter, which said thus and so. ***And that is
> his own hand, on the thus and so.** He'll not weigh it. He'll not even look up."*

**THIS DISCHARGES TOLLER'S CONDITION** — *coin, or Master Wace's own hand* — which
has been unchanged since day 42 and which he did not move.

### THE TEXT AS SENT

> **To Master Wace, steward at Draycot, by his own direction.**
>
> You granted us on the 52nd day a licence to quarry stone and take timber upon the
> crag and the waste about it, at five-and-twenty gold pieces the year, for three
> years dated from Michaelmas last, and we paid the first year in coin before you.
>
> You bade us write to you and not to Sir Aymer. We do so, and we shall do so every
> Michaelmas with the money, rendering each year what we have had off that ground.
> **This is the first such account, and we send it now rather than keep it a year,
> the first year being already gone.**
>
> **Of stone we have quarried none, and of timber we have taken none.**
>
> **What we have had off that ground is household stuff out of the ruin upon it** —
> pewter, books, a stone weight, garments, brass and copper instruments and glass,
> some coin, and a ring — **which we sold at Wethersby and at Ludbury for about four
> hundred and fifty gold pieces**, all of it before we sought your licence. **We told
> you this in the hall on the 52nd and you wrote it down as we said it.** We set it
> here so that it is in our hand as well as yours.
>
> We ask nothing of you but a receipt for this account, for our own keeping.

### THREE DELIBERATE CHOICES IN IT

| | |
|---|---|
| **"Of stone we have quarried none"** | **volunteered, and disarming.** They hold a licence to quarry and have quarried nothing, which makes the whole letter read as candour rather than cover |
| **"You wrote it down as we said it"** | **it invites him to check his own book, where the entry is.** His habit does the work |
| **OTTIR DOES NOT WITNESS IT** | Toller's call: *"a reeve's name on it makes it an act of the hundred, and then it's business. **Without him it's a tenant being tidy, and nobody reads a tenant being tidy.**"* Note this cuts the opposite way from Ottir's day-52 refinement, and correctly — **there, the party's own hand made it strangers buying stone; here, the reeve's absence keeps it domestic** |

> ### ⚠ WEND HAS NOT SEEN THIS LETTER
>
> It was drafted at Toller's shop, against her stated advice on timing, and she is
> the party's factor and the custodian of the money. **Nobody has shown it to her
> and Toller did not raise it.** Recorded as a fact, not a criticism — but it is the
> kind of thing this file exists to catch.


### ART. 11 — SIGNED, SENT, AND THE ANSWER IS IN THE POST

| | |
|---|---|

| **carried** | **WAT**, riding out on the morning of day 56, the same hour the southern cart leaves. **92** on being asked, three days after getting back off the southern road; he asked for nothing and Wend priced him at **3 s.p. a day and the hack** and entered it unasked |

## ⚠ THE FIRST FINDING RUNS AGAINST THE PARTY — day 59, Trebb and the dye at cost

**The party offered a dyer 40 gp of dye AT COST, as a kindness, after he had
identified the vial for nothing. Reaction 41** — from a man who had been at **95**
an hour earlier.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **Bern, given better tools** | **8** | badly |
| **TREBB, offered dye AT COST** | **41** | **flat** |

> ***At cost* is a DISCOUNT. A discount is a gift wearing a trade's clothes.** The
> party learned this file's first lesson from the receiving end — gifts deepen a
> debt, jobs cancel it — and then handed a proud craftsman a favour he had not
> asked for. **`articles.md` predicted the roll before it was made.**

**He took the whole of it and paid cash** *(d6 = 6)* **and then said the useful
thing:**

> *"I'll pay your cost because you've named it and I'll not haggle a man down off
> his own price. **But next year I want to buy dye off you and I want it to be
> BUSINESS.** If the first time you sold to me you did me a favour, then every time
> after that I'm a man who got a favour once. **Sell me it at your price. I'd have
> paid it.**"*

**NOTHING WAS LOST — cost was Wend's floor anyway, so the coin is identical.** What
failed to land was the intention.

> ### THE RULE FOR NEXT TIME
>
> **To a TRADESMAN, the kindness is the ORDER, not the price.** Buy from him, sell
> to him, come back next year. **A discount tells a craftsman you think he needs
> help.** Coll got **104** for being put on a roll; Bern got **68** for ongoing paid
> work and an **8** for tools; Hask got **109** for a job with coaching. **Not one
> of those was a price cut.**

**And he squared his own account in the currency a tradesman actually has** —
service, not coin. **He named the mercer**, which Pell had refused to do because he
delivers to all three: **OSSERY**, bottom of the shop street, near side of the
bridge, blue shutters — *"the one of the three who buys to sell on rather than to
sit on, and the only one with a lad who counts stock properly, so he'll know what
he's short of before you tell him."*

*"Go when he's shutting, like your carter said. **And don't offer him a favour
either.**"*

## ⚠ THE FIRST FINDING WAS INCOMPLETE — THE MISSING VARIABLE IS **WHO OWES WHOM**

**Player-caught, day 59, and the correction is to the FILE and not to the dice.**

> *"Your general rule may be true about gifts but you're always neglecting the free
> favors and help they're giving us, and we're **paying them back**."*

**Every case that built the first finding had the PARTY giving first, to someone
with nothing to return** — Bern's tools, Ista's loom, Osk's apology, Sarn's
teacher, Hask's share. **That is one direction of one axis and the file wrote it up
as a universal law.**

| direction of the debt | what a discount or a kindness IS | how it lands |
|---|---|---|
| **party gives first, NPC has nothing to return** | a **gift** — it deepens a debt that cannot be paid | **badly.** Bern 8, Ista 9, Osk 24 |
| **NPC gives first, party returns value** | **SETTLEMENT** — it discharges a debt the party owes | **it is owed, and refusing to let a man settle is the insult** |

**Trebb identified the realgar for nothing.** It made the party 35 gp and it kept
somebody from breathing arsenic in a locked room. **The party was the DEBTOR.** Dye
at cost was not charity offered downward; it was a payment.

### AND THE ACTUAL FINDING FROM DAY 59 IS A DIFFERENT ONE — **DO NOT ITEMISE A GIFT**

**Dave's reframing was right and Trebb agrees with every word of it.** He rolled
**5** anyway, and the reason is not the principle:

> **He had closed the account himself, at zero, on his own terms** — *"you've given
> me a good half hour and something to tell them about, so we're square."* **Dave
> reopened it and put a figure in it.**
>
> *"I looked at a bottle for eight minutes and I told you a word, and the word was
> worth thirty-five gold pieces... **I'd rather have not been told the figure**, and
> now I've got it, and I'll be doing sums about it at two in the morning for a
> fortnight. Thirty years I've been telling people what's in a jar for nothing.
> **Nobody's ever costed it.**"*

**A craftsman who learns he undercharged by 35 gold is not angry at the customer.
He is angry at himself** — and this is the third time in one session that a low
roll has meant exactly that:

| | |
|---|---|
| **CUDDY, 6 and 25** | *"I'm not asking to be told it wasn't a waste."* |
| **TREBB, 5** | *"I'd rather have not been told the figure."* |

> ### THE RULE
>
> **Settle the debt. Do not read out the invoice.** Pay a creditor in kind, in
> trade, in a return favour or in a price — **but do not tell him what his own
> free help was worth**, because the number is a thing he cannot give back and
> cannot forget.
>
> *(And the transaction stood: 40 gp, cash, all of it, hands shaken. Trebb had
> already named the mercer, and he will trade next year. **It was one awkward
> minute and the awkwardness was his.**)*

## 12 · THE ELEVEN — written day 65, because the count kept getting corrupted

**The DM got this wrong three times in two days, in three different directions**, and
the player settled it. **It is written here so it never has to be reconstructed
again.**

> ### **ELEVEN. TOTAL. GUARANTEED BY THE PARTY.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **guaranteed** | **all eleven** |
| **actively paid by the party right now** | **five** |
| **currently placed with MARROW** | **six** — and they are still the party's, because they went on **day 34 with a WRITTEN RIGHT OF RETURN** |
| **the mechanism** | **if Marrow runs out of work, they come back to the party.** That is what the right of return does and that is why they are on the roll |
| **the hope** | that they never need it. *"Hopefully that keeps going."* |

**So the eleven is a GUARANTEE, of which six are presently somebody else's cost.**
Both halves are true at once and neither replaces the other:

- **It is wrong to say the party pays eleven.** It pays five.
- **It is wrong to say six of them are not the party's.** They are all eleven the
  party's, and the six have a paper that says so.

### ⚠ THE NAMES — AND THE DM MUST NOT RECONSTRUCT THEM

**The composition of the eleven is written down in exactly one place: OTTIR'S
ACCOUNT OF THE HUNDRED**, where the eleven names run down the left-hand side and
where **Master Wace read them out one at a time on day 52 and argued with none of
them.** Ottir rewrote it at his own table on day 43; **the hall holds the fair copy
and Ottir holds his own.**

> **ACTION: get the eleven names off Ottir's paper and write them into this
> article.** Until that is done, this article states the STRUCTURE and not the
> membership, deliberately — **because the DM cannot enumerate them from the record
> and guessing is how the count got corrupted in the first place.**

**And this is Cuddy's counsel, which is why it works:**

> *"Put the NAMES in. Not *eleven on wages* — **the eleven names.** A list of eleven
> names is a thing he cannot argue with. **'Eleven' is a number a man can decide he
> doubts.**"*

### WHY THIS ARTICLE EXISTS AT ALL

`CLAUDE.md`'s standing doctrine: **a fact with no row, no tool and no test does not
fail loudly — it rots quietly.** The eleven had no row. It lived in three prose
files as a number in a sentence, and it drifted three times in forty-eight hours.
**An article with a list in it is a row.**
