## DAY 51, afternoon — THE SKELETONS TOLD TO CUDDY, AND HOLY WATER REFUSED

**Reaction 53 — sober, and NOT warm, and that is the right result.** Telling an
almoner there are animated dead four days up the north road is grave news and it
lands as grave news. He does not thank them for it. He goes very still and asks
short, exact questions: **how many, in what room, was the room sealed, was there
a grave, was there a stone with writing on it, and did they lie as though they had
been laid out.**

**FATHER CUDDY IS A CLERIC OF THE THIRD LEVEL** *(d6 = 3, rolled in the open)*.
He says the number himself, without being asked, because it is the answer to the
question they came with and he knows it before they finish putting it.

### THE HOLY WATER — a flat no, and it is the BOOK'S no (DMG pp. 114-115)

**Full rule and the receptacle table now cached in `rules/equipment.md`,
*Holy water*.** The three gates:

| gate | and where the party stands |
|---|---|
| **a cleric of not less than the 5th level** | **Cuddy is 3rd. Yrtone and Blartho are 1st.** Nobody within reach is close |
| **a font** — precious-metal basin in a made-to-order font, **4-10 weeks**, one per edifice | **His chapel has no font and never had one** *(d6 = 5)* — but it is a consecrated edifice in its own right, so it is **ELIGIBLE** for one. Copper is the floor: **basin 130-180 + font 200 = 330-380 gp, six vials a week** |
| **blessed crystal or leaded-glass vials**, 2-5 gp each | trivial money, and **Frython spotted that the party is sitting on ninety-seven gp of laboratory glass in area 8.** Cuddy's answer: the glass is the easy part, *the blessing is not* |

> *"Five. Not four. I have been third these eleven years and I will be third when
> they put me in the ground, and **that is not modesty, it is arithmetic.**"*

**The PHB's 25 gp a vial is a CITY price for a finished vial.** It is not
something a country chapel sells, and nothing between here and Ludbury has any.

### THE ONE LEAD — the priest at MARCHFORD

*(d6 = 3 — he can name one man and is unsure of the particulars; d6 = 5 — of the
particulars, the man's **standing** is the part he is fairly confident of.)*

**Marchford is already on the party's map**: three days south by cart, the far end
of Osk's run, where Dellie's horse was bought on day 23. Cuddy met the priest
there once, years back, and believes him to be **of standing** — fifth degree or
better.

**What he cannot vouch for is whether Marchford has a FONT.** A font is a thing a
church either has or has never been able to afford, and Marchford is a market
town, not a cathedral city. **So: a lead, not an answer.**

### AND HE WILL WRITE TO SOMEBODY — d6 = 4

**Not urgently, and NOT to the hall.** He is clear that Ottir's account and the
crag's title are the party's business and tomorrow's, and he will not put walking
dead in front of Master Wace on the morning of a petition.

**But he cannot un-know it, and he says so before anyone asks him to.** He will
write to his own superior. **He offers to show the party exactly what he writes**,
and he means the offer — this is not a threat and must not be run as one.

> *"You've told me a thing I'd rather be told than not told. I'll not repay that
> by being clever about it. **You'll read what I send.**"*

> **THE REVERSAL, and it is the whole point of the day.** He came to the morning
> office not slept and lost his place in the psalm. Then eight people asked him
> the one question only a priest can answer, and he could not answer it — and he
> was **more use in that hour than he has been in eleven years**, because he knew
> the rule, he knew his own standing, and he knew a man three days south. **A
> useless old man cannot do any of that.**

## DAY 51, EVENING — THE CRAG. THE ANSWER, AND IT IS NOT AN ANSWER

**He eats first.** Yrtone puts it in front of him and stands there until he does.
Then he washes, puts on the vestments he has not worn since the last burial, and
sits them down at the chapel table — because he said *sitting down, properly,
once*, and he meant every word of it. **Reaction 74. He does this WELL.**

### 1 · THE PERAMBULATION — d6 = 4. His own warning, come true exactly

**The crag IS named in the perambulation of Sir Aymer's waste.** Landmark to
landmark, the old way, as he said on day 41.

**And the writing does not say which side of it lies within.**

> *"I told you on the road what a marker is. I told you it may be the middle of a
> man's land or the edge of it and that the writing doesn't always trouble to say
> which. **Ten days and I have not made it say which.** I've read it forwards, I've
> read it against the two before it, and it names your hill and then it goes on to
> the next thing and it does not care, because **every man who walked those bounds
> is four hundred years dead and all of them knew.**"*

**THIS IS THE ANSWER: THE TITLE IS NOT SETTLED AND CANNOT BE SETTLED FROM THAT
CHEST.** Not his, not the party's, not nobody's. *Unresolved on the record.*

### 2 · THE OLDER MATERIAL — d6 = 5. A GRANT, AND IT NAMES THEM

Under the lord's charter, in the material he never touched, there is **a grant**.
It is legible. It is substantive. And it is the reason he could not eat today.

**It was made after the barbarians were turned back at the narrow pass** — the
battle the whole valley still half-remembers. A lord of Draycot, in his own hand,
rewarding the two men who did it.

> **ROGAHN. AND ZELLIGAR. BY NAME, IN THE LORD'S OWN CHEST.**

**The crag and the waste about it, to hold OF THE LORD, FOR THE KEEPING OF THE
NORTH WAY.**

> *"Your stories are true. That's the first thing. **The wizard and the warrior
> are not a tale, they are a line of writing in my lord's chest** and I have had
> my thumb on it for two days."*

### 3 · WHY THAT IS BOTH WORSE AND BETTER THAN *"it is his"*

| | |
|---|---|
| **WORSE** | **It proves the crag was a lord's to GRANT.** So it was never *"nobody's."* The party's whole standing position — reckoned nobody's, which is why Coll graded a cart trail up it and no one objected — **is now contradicted by a document.** `district.md` and `articles.md` both need correcting, and they now are |
| **BETTER** | **Sir Aymer's claim is by REVERSION**, not by original right. Two grantees, gone without heirs, and the land falls back in. **A reversion has to be ESTABLISHED, not assumed** — and Master Wace is precisely the sort of man who knows the difference |
| **AND** | **the grant is held FOR THE KEEPING OF THE NORTH WAY** *(d6 = 4 — the one thing in the chest that helps them)*. **Nobody has kept that way in forty years.** The party has kept it since day 6, and Ottir's account has **eleven names down the left-hand side** to prove it |

> **DO NOT MUDDLE THE CAUSATION.** The unperformed service is a **legal** fact.
> The robberies are still downstream of the layoffs, which are downstream of the
> staple moving — `district.md` stands. Two separate things that happen to point
> the same direction.

### 4 · HIS COUNSEL — and it is the same counsel as day 14, inverted

On day 14 he said *"never ask a great man for what he cannot deny you."* Tonight:

> *"**Do not raise the title.** Not a word of it, not tomorrow, not to Wace, not to
> Perrin, not in that hall at all. **A steward asked to rule on a thing he'd have
> to research will say no while he researches**, and he'll say it for a year."*
>
> *"Ask for the **licence**. The quarry and the timber, the five-and-twenty a year,
> the three years, the coin on the day, exactly as your Wend has written it. **A
> licence concedes it is his to license** — and that is the cheapest thing you will
> ever be asked to concede, and you get a paper in his hand that says you may be
> up there. **Lead with the road.** I told you that too."*

*(d6 = 5 — and he is likely right. Wace trusts paper, the paper is genuinely
unclear, and a steward facing an unclear record, a cleared road and 25 gp a year
offered in writing takes the licence rather than opens a suit.)*

### 5 · THE HARD THING, said plainly

**They have taken about 450 gp of goods off a hill that may be the lord's**, and
that money bought the clip. **The licence is retrospective cover** and Wend's
second letter already names the position exactly: *a tenant who exceeded his
terms.* He does not soften it.

> *"You'll not be hanged for it. You may be **charged** for it. Better charged as
> a tenant than found as a trespasser, and better this year than next."*

### 6 · WHAT HE HAS MADE THEM — and what he says to do with it

**He has copied the operative words of the grant out in his own hand**, on his own
paper, with the names and the service in it. Ten days and a pen and an almoner.

**AND HE TELLS THEM NOT TO USE IT TOMORROW.**

> *"You'll want to put that on the table. **Don't.** It's the one paper in the
> world that proves the hill was a lord's to give away, and the man you'd be
> handing it to is the lord's steward. **You'd be arming him.**"*
>
> *"Keep it. Read it. **Know a thing without spending it** — you're better at that
> than most, I've watched you do it with the dealer. But know it."*

### 7 · WHAT HE CANNOT DO, and says so for the third time today

> *"I can read. **I cannot rule.** I'm an almoner and there is a difference between
> a man who has read the writings and a man who is entitled to say what they mean,
> and everything I've told you tonight is the first kind."*

**Three questions asked of him today by people who treated him as a priest, and
he could answer none of them cleanly: the holy water, the dwarf, and the hill.**
He knew the rule on the first, he knew his own limits on the second, and on the
third he did ten days' work and came back with *"it does not say."*

> **AND THAT IS THE MOST USEFUL DAY ANYONE HAS HAD OUT OF FATHER CUDDY IN ELEVEN
> YEARS.** An unclear answer that he can prove is unclear is worth more than a
> confident wrong one, and it is the entire reason the party gets to walk into
> that hall tomorrow and ask for the small thing instead of the large one.


### DAY 51, last thing — HE IS THANKED, AND THEN THE ALMONER ASKS

*"Thank you Cuddy, we really appreciate all the work you did to figure this out.
If there's anything we can do to help you anytime, let us know."*

**Reaction 81 — the THANKS land.** And there is a reason they land when the
identical shape of offer has flopped repeatedly in this campaign:

> **REFINEMENT OF THE SECOND FINDING (`articles.md`).** *"Anything we can do,
> anytime"* is **approval without a day and a name in it** — the exact shape that
> got Sarn's **42** on day 39. It got **81** here, and the difference is not the
> room and not the wording. **Sarn was indebted. Cuddy is OWED.** He had just done
> ten days of work these people watched him do, so being thanked settles an account
> instead of opening one, and the vague offer rides along on the thanks rather than
> carrying the scene. **The finding stands: a debtor needs an hour and a job. A
> CREDITOR just needs to be thanked properly, and this was.**

### AND HE ASKS FOR SOMETHING — d6 = 6, clearly, without hedging

**This is a reversal of type and should be played as one.** He has been the man who
gives for sixty years; it is the office. He puts his hands flat on the table.

> *"Yes. There is. I'd not have said so a fortnight ago and I'm going to say it
> now, so let me get it out in one go."*
>
> *"**Send me word.** In writing, here, to the chapel. Four things and I'll not
> ask for a fifth: **when you know about Marchford. When the dwarf is in the
> ground. When the spinner comes north. And when the licence is granted or it
> isn't.** I'll answer every one of them, which is worth nothing to you, but I'll
> answer them."*
>
> *"I sat at that table thirty years and never once knew anything I said to leave
> the room. **Something did. I should like to know how it comes out.** That's the
> whole of the ask and I know it's a great deal to put on people with a road to
> walk."*

**IT COSTS THE PARTY ALMOST NOTHING AND IT IS THE ONE THING HE ACTUALLY LACKS.**
Take it seriously in the log: **four named notifications, and he is owed all four.**

### The hope he says once and does not press

**He would like to be at the dwarf's burial** — and says in the same breath that
he cannot walk it, that he knows what it would cost them to fetch an old man four
days north and four days back, and that they are to forget he mentioned it if it
does not fit.

> *"I'd not ask. I'm only telling you I'd come."*

### What he does NOT ask for — d6 = 4, and it is visible to anyone who looks

**The chapel roof is bad** at the north end, patched with turf, and it is going to
be a wet winter. **And an almoner's burial purse comes from the hall and is empty
by Candlemas most years** — he has buried people at his own cost and does not
mention it, and it took no detective work to see it: the man had no supper in the
house.

**HE DID NOT ASK FOR EITHER AND WILL NOT.** If the party moves on it, remember
what `articles.md` says: **money handed to a man who cannot repay it deepens the
debt.** A roof mended by Coll's crew on a passing job, or a standing sum entered on
Wend's books as *the Draycot burial account* with his name on it as the man who
DISBURSES it, is work. **A purse pressed across a table is a gift.**

### AND HE DOES NOT THINK OF THE MARCHFORD LETTER — d6 = 2

**A letter of introduction from one priest to another is the single most valuable
thing he owns and it does not occur to him.** He gave them the name and considers
that discharged.

> **DM: DO NOT HAND THIS OVER.** If the party wants an introduction to the

## ⚠ THE LETTER PROBLEM — WEND WROTE A BINARY AND THE ANSWER IS NEITHER

**Article 9 offers LETTER A (*the crag is NOT his* — say nothing) or LETTER B
(*the crag IS his* — petition the licence). Cuddy's answer is that it is NOT
SETTLED.** So on its own terms neither letter fits, and one of them burns tonight.

**The case for B is strong and comes from BOTH advisers independently:**

- **Wend's own exposure paragraph kills A.** Anybody who rides out to that crag
  finds a graded cart trail, a levelled flat, a fire pit, a latrine, **a boxed
  spring** and a lean-to that sleeps eight. *"It is not possible to pretend nobody
  has been using that hill. The licence has to be the explanation, or there isn't
  one."* **Saying nothing is not a neutral act any more.**
- **Cuddy said take it tonight.** A licence concedes it is his to license — the
  cheapest concession available — and a steward asked to *rule* on an unclear
  record says no for a year while he researches.
- **And the record is now known to be unclear, which is a reason to settle rather
  than to argue.** Ambiguity favours whoever is holding the paper at the end.

**The case against B:** it concedes a point that is **not proven**, in writing,
against a claim that runs only by **reversion** — and it does so the morning after
learning the crag was granted out once already, for a service the party is the only
people in forty years to have performed.

**A third road nobody wrote:** petition for a licence over *the waste about the
crag* without naming the crag as within it. **Cuddy's likely view, unrolled and
therefore not his word yet: a steward smells that, and cleverness in a petition is
what makes a man send somebody to look.**

> **THIS IS THE PARTY'S DECISION AND IT IS THE HINGE OF THE WHOLE ARC.** Do not
> make it for them.


### OTTIR BRIEFED, night of day 51 — *"just tell the truth, and we'll back you up"*

**Reaction 81.** The party's line: tell the truth, we stand behind you, **and if he
asks where the money came from, WE will tell him.**

**The load-bearing part is the last clause and it is not the reassurance — it is
the TRANSFER.** Ottir has spent eleven years being the man who has to decide what
to say to a household he is frightened of, and the party has just taken the one
decision he could not make off him entirely. **Same mechanism as the surety he
"took with relief" on day 4.** He does not make a speech about it.

> *"Right. **Right.** That's — yes. I can do the truth. I've been doing the truth
> for eleven years, it's the deciding that I've never once been any good at."*

**He checks once, because he is a reeve and he has to:** do they understand that
they are offering to stand in a lord's hall and say they carried goods off the
lord's hill. **They do.** He does not ask twice.

### HIS REFINEMENT — d6 = 5, and nobody in the party could have known it

> **THE PETITION AND THE COIN GO ON THAT TABLE *BEFORE* THE MONEY QUESTION IS

> *"Listen. **It's the same sentence either way and it lands two different ways.**

**This is Wend's *"better charged as a tenant than found as a trespasser"* turned
