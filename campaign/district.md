# The district — setting built at the table, day 6 after expedition 4

**B1 supplies a crag and a dungeon and NOT ONE WORD about the surrounding
country.** Everything here was generated in play, mostly on dice, when the party
started asking questions the module does not answer. It is written down so it
stays fixed and the player can hold me to it. **Nothing here is DM-side secret.**

**So the chain of leads is:** ask **Sir Aymer** (a day's ride, and a man who sells

there. Neither is a guess; both are real and neither has been walked.

**The party came in from the south-WEST and the wool went SOUTH.** Their own road
knowledge does not help.

## What the land does

Uplands are **sheep**. Lower ground is grain and some cattle. The town is a market
town on the **north road**, a reeve's town answering to a **distant lord** seen
twice a year.

## Why everyone shed hands in the same season — d6 = 1

**The buyers changed. The wool staple moved and the big buyer stopped coming this
far up.**

This is the single most important fact in the district and it is **not** a
production problem:

- **The wool still exists.** The grass still grows, the sheep still would.
- **Nobody is coming to buy it here any more.**
- So the price collapsed locally, flocks were sold off, and every holding that
  was carrying hands for shearing and winter work stopped carrying them —
  **all at once, at Michaelmas, for the same reason.**

Rell's brother turning off eleven with nothing written down is a nastier version
of the same thing, not a separate cause.

**Consequence: the robberies are DOWNSTREAM.** Hask's four and Coll and Ferd are
all the same event, months apart. The road did not cause this and patrolling the
road cannot end it.

## Is there land to work? — d6 = 5. NO

**Every acre is held and used.** There is no idle holding, no abandoned farm, no
waste going begging.

- **Assarting is dead.** Clearing woodland means the *waste*, the waste belongs to
  the lord, and **Ottir cannot grant a licence** — he is a reeve, not a bailiff
  with seisin. That question goes to a man seen twice a year.
- **A timber business is dead for the same reason.** The woods are the lord's.

**These were good ideas and the land says no.** Do not soften it; the answer
points somewhere better.

## WHERE IT POINTS — transport and market access, not land

The wool exists and the buyer stopped coming. **Anyone who could get the clip to
where the buyers now are would fix the whole thing.** That needs:

| need | who has it |
|---|---|
| knowledge of the trade, and the contacts at the far end | **WEND** — cloth and wool, off the market square |
| carriage | **HALM** — new mule, sound cart, and nothing to haul |
| capital | **THE PARTY** — 630 gp |
| labour | **eight men already taken on, and about a dozen more out there** |

**Bern has NO ROOM — d6 = 6.** A brand-new shop in a poor town can barely keep
Corm busy, wedding commission or not. The joiner is not the answer to eight men
and the party should be told so plainly when they ask.
| a road that is demonstrably safe | **the patrol week, and Osk the carrier saying so unprompted** |

**Nobody has assembled this and the party has not seen it yet.** Do not point at
it. Ottir will mention Wend as a fact about wool without meaning anything by it.

**Note what it does to Wend.** Her grievance is that she was managed around and
made to look a fool in public. The one thing that would answer it is being made
**central** to something, in the open, in front of the town.

*(Written before she was asked, on the assumption she would know where the buyers
went. **She does not** — see the next section. Left standing as written so the
guess and the roll that overturned it are both on the record.)*

## What Wend actually knows — and the one thing she does not

Rolled day 6 in her shop, after she came round (baseline **115** before anyone
spoke, then **130** on the caravan proposal — she is all the way in).

| question | roll | answer |
|---|---|---|
| Does she know where the wool staple moved to? | **d6 = 6** | **NO. She does not know either.** |
| Where did the sold-off flocks go? | **d6 = 2** | **Bought up cheap by the bigger holders. THE SHEEP ARE STILL IN THE DISTRICT.** |

**So the venture is real and the last piece is missing:**

- **This summer's clip is sitting in barns**, unsold, because the buyer never came.
  That follows directly from the staple moving; it is not a separate fact.
- **The flocks are still here**, concentrated in fewer hands — and those hands are
  now sitting on wool they cannot sell either. They will deal.
- **Wend knows the trade**: grading, weights, what a buyer pays, and who in the
  district has wool in a barn right now.
- **Wend has no capital.** She is a small trader who lost a bolt of cloth last
  week and it mattered.
- **NOBODY KNOWS WHERE TO TAKE IT.** You cannot sell wool to a place you cannot
  name, and it is not a thing anyone learns by asking in this town.

## LUDBURY — ANSWERED, day 14, by Father Cuddy at Draycot

**The wool goes to LUDBURY. Five days south — three to Marchford, two beyond it.**

Sir Aymer's clip has gone there **these two summers**. Cuddy has heard *"the
Ludbury factor this, the Ludbury price that"* at that table a dozen times.
**d6 = 1: he knows and can name it.**

### Two answers that go with it

| question | roll | answer |
|---|---|---|
| Why was Wethersby never told? | **d6 = 3** | **It never occurred to Sir Aymer.** No scheme, nothing gained — his clip goes to Ludbury either way and one more seller will not move the price a farthing. **Nobody up here has thought about that town at all, for two years.** Cuddy is ashamed, because he sat at the table and it never crossed his mind either |
| Does Sir Aymer hold any right over goods leaving his hundred? | **d6 = 5** | **NOTHING.** No toll, no market franchise, nothing enforceable. Cuddy has read the writings in the chest. **The party needs no leave to take Wethersby's wool to Ludbury** |

**Cuddy's counsel, and it is good:** *"Never ask a great man for what he cannot
deny you. He'll deny it anyway, out of surprise."*

### What still needs Sir Aymer

| ask | needs him? |
|---|---|
| Sell Wethersby's clip at Ludbury | **No.** Go and do it |
| Mend the causeway and the bridge | **Yes** — his road, and Ottir will not lift a spade without his word |
| Break the waste for new farmland | **Yes**, and it is the largest ask |

**Master Wace's next day is the best part of a week off.** The party chose to
defer both asks rather than press an advantage the same afternoon Cuddy gave them
Ludbury. **He noticed.**

## The convergence — do NOT point at it

Finding the staple means **going south to a city and asking**. The party already
has a separate reason to go to a city: **Toller said the diary would fetch a
great deal more from a collector there and offered to broker it for a cut.**

One journey answers both. They also have a carter with a cart and nothing to haul,
eight men on the books, and a road they have just spent a week proving is safe.

**Let them assemble it. Say nothing.**

## THE CRAG WAS NEVER "NOBODY'S" — CORRECTED day 51

**The district's belief, and the party's standing position, was that the crag was
reckoned nobody's — which is why Coll graded a cart-passable trail up it and
nobody objected.** That belief is *what a district says.* It is now contradicted
by a document.

**Cuddy's ten days in the chest (day 51):**

- The crag **is** named in the perambulation of Sir Aymer's waste, as a **marker**,
  and the writing does not say which side lies within. **Genuinely unresolved.**
- **Beneath the charter there is a GRANT naming ROGAHN and ZELLIGAR** — the crag
  and the waste about it, **to hold of the lord, for the keeping of the north
  way** — made after the barbarians were turned back at the narrow pass.
- **Therefore the crag was a lord's to grant.** Sir Aymer's claim runs by
  **reversion** (grantees dead without heirs), which must be established.

> **THE CAUSATION IN THIS FILE STANDS UNCHANGED.** The robberies are downstream
> of the layoffs, which are downstream of **the wool staple moving** (d6 = 1, day
> 6). The forty-year lapse of the grant's *service* is a **legal** fact and is NOT
> a second cause of the robberies. Two separate things that happen to point the
> same way. Do not merge them.
