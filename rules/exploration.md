# Exploration, Light & Time — AD&D 1e

## Light sources (PHB p. 102, verified)

| Source | Radius | Burning time |
|---|---|---|
| **Torch** | **40'** | **6 turns (1 hour)** |
| **Lantern (hooded)** | **30'** | **24 turns** per pint of oil |
| Bullseye lanthorn | 80' | 24 turns per pint — 1" wide beam only, shutterable |
| Magic dagger | 10' | infinite |
| Magic short sword | 15' | infinite |
| Magic long sword | 20' | infinite |

*source: **PHB p. 102**, the printed light-source list; transcribed and marked verified when this cache was written. `tools/light.py` reads its burn times from this table and nowhere else.*

**Read the Burning time column literally — `tools/light.py` keys off its wording.**
"24 turns **per pint of oil**" means the fuel is separate from the fitting, so the
source is **refillable**; "6 turns (1 hour)" names no fuel, so a torch is a
**consumed item** and cannot be refilled; "infinite" never burns down at all.

**The lantern is far more efficient:** 30' vs 40' radius, but **4× the burn time** per flask.
Party carries 2 hooded lanterns and 6 flasks of oil = 144 turns of light, plus 6 torches at
6 turns each = 36 turns. Roughly **30 hours** of light total if managed well.

Carrying a lantern or torch **occupies one hand** — no bow, no two-handed weapon.

**Infravision** (Tristle, 60') does not function while a light source is active nearby; it
reads heat, and light washes it out.

## HOUSE RULES

These are DM rulings where the PHB/DMG are silent. Verified absent from the books, not
inventions layered over existing rules.

### Movement — three speeds, not two (PHB p. 102, VERIFIED)

> *"The movement distance in the dungeon is 1" to 10' over a turn of 10 minutes
> duration while exploration and mapping are in progress. If the party is
> following a known route or map, the movement rate is **5 times greater**, so
> each move takes 1/5 of a turn (2 rounds). If the party is fleeing, all
> movement — excluding encumbered movement, is **10 times faster**, so each move
> takes only 1/10 of a turn, or 1 round. **This same movement rate applies to
> combat situations.**"*
>
> — **PHB p. 102**, MOVEMENT — TIME AND DISTANCE FACTORS, text layer, 2026-08-27.

| Movement rate | per ROUND (fleeing / combat) | per SEGMENT |
|---|---|---|
| 6" | 60' | 6' |
| 9" | 90' | 9' |
| 12" | 120' | 12' |
| 15" | 150' | 15' |
| 18" | 180' | 18' |

*source: **PHB p. 102**, the printed table, 2026-08-27.*

So for **this party at 9"** (four in banded mail set the pace):

| Situation | Speed | 50 feet costs |
|---|---|---|
| Exploring and mapping | 90 ft per **turn** | 6 rounds |
| **Following a known route** | 90 ft per **2 rounds** — *5× faster* | **2 rounds** |
| Fleeing, or in combat | 90 ft per **round** | 1 round |

**ERROR DISCLOSED, 2026-08-27.** Everything up to expedition 4 round 90 charged
known ground at roughly 120 ft per turn — the middle speed was missing
entirely, and known-route movement was run about **four to five times too slow**.
Roughly **20 rounds** of expedition 4 were overcharged.

**The rounds already played stand.** Wandering checks fired at rounds 21, 41, 64
and 81 on the old clock and one of them produced a troglodyte that fought and
died; unwinding that would be worse than the error. This follows the precedent
set when the wandering-check rate itself was audited: *"Encounters generated by
the old method stand; the procedure is fixed going forward."*

**No mapping is possible at the fast speeds** — the PHB says so directly. A
party that runs does not map, and a party following a known route is following
a map it already has.

### Lighting a light source

*(No lighting time is given anywhere in the DMG — checked.)*

| Situation | Time |
|---|---|
| From an existing flame (lit torch/lantern, incl. a hooded one) | **1 segment** — effectively instant |
| From cold, flint & steel + dry tinder, unhurried | **1 full round**, doing nothing else |
| From cold while hurried, wet, or in a draught | **2–3 rounds**, plus a roll |

**Consequence: you cannot light a light source during combat.** Light is arranged before
trouble or not at all.

### Hooded lanterns and infravision

- A **hooded lantern can be fully shuttered** — lit inside, showing no light. This is what
  "hooded" means and it is free to do.
- **HOODING DOES NOT STOP THE BURN.** The flame is still lit behind the shutter; the shutter
  hides the light, it does not starve the wick. A hooded lantern therefore consumes oil at
  exactly the same rate as an open one, and `tools/light.py` charges it accordingly. This is
  the conservative reading and it is the same fact the infravision ruling below turns on: the
  lamp is a heat source *because it is still burning*.
- **A hooded lantern does NOT enable infravision.** Infravision reads heat; a burning lamp
  is a heat source whether or not light escapes. Anyone within roughly **30 feet** of a lit
  lantern — hooded or open — has their infravision washed out.
- Therefore: hooding buys **light discipline and instant relighting**, not heat-sight. To
  use infravision, the source must be **extinguished** or the viewer must separate from it.

### Troglodyte camouflage vs. infravision

Troglodyte chameleon skin gives surprise on 1–4 **by sight only**. It conceals nothing from
infravision — it cannot hide body heat. In total darkness a troglodyte is fully visible to
an infravision user and loses its surprise bonus entirely.

### Missile fire into a melee

**SCOPE — read this first. Everything in this section requires an ACTUAL MELEE:
allies and enemies in contact, intermingled.** If nothing is engaged, none of it
applies and a missile attack is resolved normally, with range modifiers only.

| Situation | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Shooting into a melee, allies not directly in the lane | **natural 1** strikes a friendly at random among those engaged. No other penalty. |
| Shooting into a melee **past allies who fill a 10' corridor** | **−4, and on a miss a 50% chance of hitting the ally in front** — usually not worth attempting |
| **No melee** — enemies at a distance, nobody in contact | **no penalty at all.** Range modifiers only. Allies merely *standing* in front are not a melee. |

**Both misreadings have now happened, in opposite directions:**

- **Round 397** — Frython's darts were resolved with no penalty while *"the rats
  had won initiative and were already in contact."* That was a melee; the throws
  were retracted.
- **Round 1203** — the corridor penalty was about to be applied to a throw at rats
  **20 feet away with nobody engaged.** The player caught it: *"the rule about
  hitting people in the party in front of you is when they're in melee, not just
  when they are standing in front."* Correct. No penalty was applied.

The test is **contact, not geometry.** Ask "is anyone actually engaged?" before
asking "who is in the lane?"

### Withdrawing from melee

A fighter who falls back while an ally immediately fills the gap takes **no free attack
against them** — that's a relief in good order. A creature that **breaks and flees** does
give free attacks, at **+2** for showing its back.

### Recoverable ammunition

Darts, arrows, and sling bullets are recoverable after a fight in a stone dungeon. 1e gives
no breakage rule for them and none is imposed.

## Encumbrance (PHB pp. 101-102, verified from page renders)

Weight is stated in **gold pieces; 10 gp = 1 pound**.

| Encumbrance | Movement | Reaction / Initiative |
|---|---|---|
| **normal gear** — about 35# (350 gp), no great bulk | **12"** — can run quickly | normal or better |
| **heavy gear** — armor and/or equipment about 70# (700 gp), or fairly bulky | **9"** — a lumbering run | normal, no bonuses |
| **very heavy gear** — 105# (1050 gp) and bulky (e.g. plate) | **6"** — can trot for short distances | slowed |
| **encumbered** — over 105# and/or very bulky | **3"-4"** — no trotting | slowed greatly |

"Strength penalties or bonuses will modify these guidelines" — the allowance from
STRENGTH TABLE II is **added to each band**.

| STR | Weight allowance | | STR | Weight allowance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | −350 | | 14–15 | +200 |
| 4–5 | −250 | | 16 | +350 |
| 6–7 | −150 | | 17 | +500 |
| **8–9** | **normal (no penalty)** | | 18 | +750 |
| 10–11 | normal | | 18/01–50 | +1,000 |
| 12–13 | +100 | | 18/51–75 | +1,250 |

**Note the boundary: 8–9 is *normal*, not a penalty.** Only 3–7 are penalised.

**Bulk is separate from weight and is a DM ruling.** The PHB is explicit that it
cannot be computed — and its own worked example is, of all things, *"how big is a
tapestry?"* (p. 102). A load that is awkward rather than heavy still costs a band.

## Time

- 1 turn = **10 minutes**. 1 melee round = 1 minute (10 rounds/turn), 10 segments/round.
- Searching a 10' section for traps or secret doors: **1 turn each**.
- Movement 9" = 90 feet per turn cautiously mapping, faster if not mapping.

### What is NOT on this list, and stays open

reaction rolls all stay VISIBLE**, with their modifiers and sources. Being
caught flat-footed, a creature breaking and running, a blow landing, a save

| Race | Chance |
|---|---|
| Gnome | 4 in 20 (20%) |
| Elf | 3 in 20 (**15%**) |
| Halfling | 3 in 20 (15%) |
| Half-orc | 3 in 20 (15%) |
| Dwarf | 2 in 20 (10%) |
| Half-elf | 2 in 20 (10%) |

## TIME AND WANDERING CHECKS — the procedure

**Track elapsed time in ROUNDS.** 1 round = 1 minute. 10 rounds = 1 turn.

**Roll one wandering check per interval, 1 in 6.** Not per action, not per corridor segment,
not per narrative beat.

> *"Every third turn of adventuring, the DM should take a die roll for the possible
> appearance of wandering monsters at the indicated chances (which are normally 1 in 6, but
> which may vary depending upon location and dungeon level). Some occurrences (such as noise
> and commotion caused by adventurers) may necessitate additional checks."*
> — **B1 p. 4**, TIME, 200 dpi page render 2026-08-30

**Every THIRD turn is 30 rounds.** Checks anchor at every multiple of **30** rounds — 30, 60,
90, 120… **Each fires at its anchor + (1d6 − 1)**, i.e. somewhere in the five rounds
following the mark.

Average spacing is therefore exactly **30 rounds** — the module's rate — while the moment of
firing is unpredictable.

### Carrying a litter — DM ruling, 2026-08-30

**A party carrying a casualty on a litter moves at HALF the known-route rate.**
Two of its number have both hands full and cannot fight, the litter cannot corner
quickly, and the pace is set by not jolting the man on it. For this 9" party that
is roughly **225 ft per turn** instead of 450 — about 22 ft a round.

**Do not charge the exploring rate for it.** Exploring is 90 ft/turn and that
already prices in mapping, listening and checking walls; a party retracing a route
it walked yesterday is doing none of those things, litter or no litter.

**This was got wrong immediately after being written down.** The march from the
west junction to the four-way — 120 ft of ground the party had walked the day
before — was charged **13 rounds**, the exploring rate. At the correct
litter-paced rate it is **five or six**. Nothing came of it (the checks in those
rounds fired empty), and the rounds stand per the standing precedent, but
over-charging time is not the safe direction to err in: **every extra round is
extra wandering checks**, which is the exact complaint the player raised two
exchanges earlier. Erring slow feels cautious and is not.

`tools/party.py move --rate known` prints the honest number. Halve it for a
litter and use that.

### RESOLVE A CHECK AT THE ROUND IT FIRES, OR NOT AT ALL — never bank one

**A wandering check is resolved at the round it fires, at wherever the party is
at that round.** If the creature cannot reach them then, the check is **spent**:
it wandered somewhere else and they never knew it was there. It does not get held
over.

**Banking a rolled monster and placing it in the party's path later is the single
most distorting thing a DM can do with this clock**, and it is invisible in the
log, because the log records the round the check *fired*, not the round the
encounter was *narrated*.

What it costs:

- It converts a check the party effectively survived into a guaranteed encounter,
  at a moment of the DM's choosing rather than the dice's.
- It **collapses the interval**. Two checks 39 rounds apart become two encounters
  two rounds apart, which is exactly what the 30-round anchor exists to prevent.
- It is usually a **movement-rate absurdity**. Most wandering monsters move faster
  than the party. A giant centipede is **MV 15"** — 150 feet in a turn. It will not
  wait four turns on one flagstone for a door to open.

**Caught 2026-08-30 by the player.** The round-2183 check produced a giant
centipede while the party was sealed in area 5 at breakfast. Correct resolution:
it is in the corridor, it cannot reach them, **the check is spent and the corridor
is empty when they open the door**. What actually happened: it was parked at
(21,23) and met the party at round 2220, two rounds before the kobolds fired at
2222. The player's objection — *"you're claiming 40 rounds elapsed when in reality
it was 2-3"* — is exactly right, and no amount of correcting the interval fixes it,
because the interval was never the mechanism at fault.

**The time charged was NOT the error.** Thirty rounds for Yrtone's prayer is DMG
p.40; breakfast and packing on top of it is fair. A DM tempted to shave the clock
after an incident like this is fixing the wrong thing and will end up running the
dungeon too fast to be dangerous.

**The kobolds at 2222 are the shape every check should have:** fired, party in an
open corridor, approach verified, resolved on the spot, narrated in the round it
happened.

### THE INTERVAL WAS 20 FOR THE WHOLE CAMPAIGN AND THAT WAS WRONG

This file used to say *"Checks anchor at every multiple of 20 rounds… average spacing is
therefore exactly 20 rounds — the module's rate"*, and `tools/clock.py` had `ANCHOR = 20` to
match. **The module's rate is 30.** The claim that 20 was "the module's rate" carried no page
citation, which is precisely the failure mode the provenance lines in these files exist to
catch — an uncited number sat in the cache for the entire campaign and the code trusted it.

The cost: about **1.5× the intended encounter rate** — one encounter per ~120 rounds of
dungeon time instead of per ~180. Roughly 110 checks were rolled where the module wanted 73.

**Found 2026-08-30 because the player asked why two encounters had come back to back.** They
had not; the mechanism was working exactly as specified. The *specification* was wrong.

**Encounters already generated STAND**, per the precedent set when the movement rates were
audited: *"Encounters generated by the old method stand; the procedure is fixed going
forward."* Unwinding a troglodyte that fought and died would be worse than the error.

`clock.py reanchor --why "..."` retires every unfired future anchor and rolls a fresh hidden
series at the new interval. It exists because changing `ANCHOR` alone does **not** change the
schedule — the anchors live in `campaign/clock.tsv`, and the stale ones go on firing forever. **Keep the jitter roll hidden in advance**; report it afterward
(*"the round-120 check fired at 123"*) so the clock stays auditable without being gameable.
Adopted 2026-08-18 at the player's suggestion.

### Extra checks — only these

Genuinely loud events, which the module sanctions: hammering spikes, smashing furniture or
doors, the magic mouths firing, a shouted fight. **One extra check, not one per action.**

### Wilderness travel — NO random encounters

**Travel between town and Quasqueton is narrated.** It costs the days and the rations. **No
random encounter rolls, in either direction.**

Reasons, in order of weight:
1. **Consistency.** The first four journeys were run this way. Introducing the risk mid-way —
   and specifically on a retreat with the party half-dead — was inconsistent and unfair. It
   happened once, on 2026-08-18, and the encounter was voided.
2. **The PHB says wilderness is higher-level content**: *"Adventuring into unknown lands or
   howling wilderness is extremely perilous at best… Protracted expeditions are, therefore,
   normally undertaken by higher level characters."* (PHB p.101.)
3. **B1 is a dungeon module.** It provides no wilderness tables. The tension belongs
   underground, where the stocking and the procedures actually live.

Revisit only at the **start** of an expedition and only by agreement — never mid-journey.

### Placement rule

The die tells me **what**, never **where**. If the rolled direction puts a creature somewhere
it could not physically have come from — a sealed room, a dead-end limb with no other exit,
a corridor the party has bottled — **relocate it to a plausible approach or void the
encounter.** Do not invent geography to justify a roll.

*Adopted 2026-08-18 after an audit found checks were being rolled 2–5× the correct rate on
travel. Encounters generated by the old method stand; the procedure is fixed going forward.*

### Opening a door in B1 — the MODULE'S rule, not the PHB's

> *"There are many doors within the dungeon… In all cases, unless otherwise
> noted, **doors will be locked one-third of the time** — and any roll of a 1 or
> 2 on a six-sided die (d6) will mean that they will bar entrance unless the lock
> is sprung or broken. Breaking the lock or breaking down the entire door will be
> a noisy undertaking, to be sure, and may serve to attract unwelcome monsters."*
>
> — **B1, introduction**, module text, 2026-08-28.

| d6, rolled ONCE per door | Result |
|---|---|
| **1-2** | **Locked.** It bars entrance until picked or broken. |
| **3-6** | **It opens.** No roll, no Strength check, no drama. |

*source: **B1 module introduction**, quoted above.*

**A locked door is opened by:**
- **Tristle's Open Locks — 35%** (base 25 + elf -5 + DEX 18 +15), one attempt per
  lock per level of experience
- or **breaking it**, which the module says is noisy and *"may serve to attract
  unwelcome monsters"* — that is an extra wandering check, by the module's own
  words

**THE PHB OPEN-DOORS ROLL DOES NOT APPLY TO ORDINARY B1 DOORS.** PHB p.9 says
that roll is for *"a stuck or heavy door"*. B1 does not describe its doors as
stuck; it describes a third of them as locked. Use the module.

**ERROR DISCLOSED 2026-08-28.** Every door in expedition 4 was resolved with a
Strength open-doors check — Torsat at 1-2 in 6, Dave at 1-3 — and several took
five or six attempts. Under the module's actual rule most of them would simply
have opened. The doors already opened STAND (they were played), following the
precedent set when the wandering-check rate was audited, but the procedure is
fixed from here.

### Two people forcing a door together

**DM RULING 2026-08-28. No rule found in PHB or DMG.** A second person shoving
or hauling alongside adds **one step to the open-doors range** — Torsat's 1-2
becomes 1-3, Dave's 1-3 becomes 1-4.

**It does not stack past two.** A third and fourth body cannot get purchase on
the same leaf, and without a cap six characters would open every door in the
dungeon automatically, which is plainly not the intent of a table that runs from
1 to 5 in 6 across the entire Strength range.

Use the **better** character's range as the base and add one.

### Spiking a door — NOISE. Standing ruling, settled 2026-08-29

**Setting or drawing spikes deliberately and quietly costs NO check and NO roll.**
Not a wandering check, not a listen roll, nothing. It is ambient noise.

| What is being done | Noise consequence |
|---|---|
| Spikes eased in or prised out, by hand and mallet-handle, taking the time | **nothing. No roll.** |
| The same, but with a KNOWN creature already established within earshot | roll whether *that creature* hears it — this is not a wandering check |
| Spikes hammered fast, under pressure, with something hauling on the far side | noisy; DM call, an extra check is fair |
| **Breaking a lock, or breaking down a door** | **noisy by the module's own words — extra wandering check** |

*source: reasoned from **B1's front matter**, which singles out one act only —
*"Breaking the lock or breaking down the entire door will be a noisy undertaking,
to be sure, and may serve to attract unwelcome monsters"* — and from **PHB p. 9**,
whose noise clause attaches to *forcing* a stuck door ("each such attempt requires
time and makes considerable noise"), not to blocking one. Nothing in either book
makes spiking noisy.*

**Why this is written down.** The correct ruling was already made in play at the
trophy room — *"done quietly by hand and mallet-handle rather than hammered, so
no extra wandering check"* — and then drifted: three routine noise rolls were made
on 2026-08-29 (rounds 113, 115, 117) with no listener established anywhere. All
three came up unheard, so nothing turned on them, but the drift is the point. The
player caught it. **A ruling made once and not cached is a ruling that will be
re-made differently.**

## Spiking a door — which way it opens decides the method

**A door that opens TOWARD you can be spiked into the floor**, driving wedges in
front of the leaf so it physically cannot swing. This is much stronger than
anything done to the jamb: the spikes are in compression against the whole
weight of the door and the floor takes the load.

**A door that opens AWAY from you cannot.** Floor spikes on your side do nothing
— the leaf just swings away from them. All you can do is **wedge the jamb gap**,
which jams the leaf in its frame and works in either direction but is weaker.

| Door opens | Method | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Toward you | wedges driven into the floor in front of the leaf | **strong** |
| Away from you | wedges hammered into the jamb gap | weaker |

troglodytes battering it. The **worship area** door (26,8,V) opens toward the
party, so its spikes go into the floor and are worth considerably more.

## Which way a door swings decides TWO things, not one

The swing direction was already load-bearing for spiking. **It is equally
load-bearing for oiling, and that was missed once.**

| the door… | spiking it from your side | **oiling the hinges from your side** |
|---|---|---|
| **opens TOWARD you** | **FLOOR spikes** — the strong method; the leaf is driven onto them | **YES.** The hinge knuckles and pins are on your face of the frame, in reach |
| **opens AWAY from you** | **JAMB wedges** — the weaker method; floor spikes cannot hold it | **NO. THE HINGES ARE ON THE FAR SIDE.** There is nothing on your side to oil |

*source: **NOT A BOOK TABLE.** Neither the PHB nor the DMG says a word about
hinge sides. This is physical fact plus a DM ruling, set 2026-09-03, and the
spiking half of it is the same ruling already used since expedition 4. Recorded
here so it is applied consistently and is visibly a ruling rather than a rule.*

**And this is also how you TELL which way it swings without touching it:** if you
can see the hinge knuckles, it opens toward you. If you can see the stop or
rebate and no hardware, it opens away.

### SPIKES ARE VISIBLE FROM THE SIDE YOU DRIVE THEM ON

**Player's point, 2026-09-04, and it changes when spiking is worth doing.**

**Floor spikes lie on the floor in front of the leaf, on your side.** Jamb wedges
stand proud of the frame, likewise on your side. Either way, **anyone who walks
up to that door sees them** — and anything with the wit to try a latch has the
wit to read two iron wedges hammered into the stone.

| you spike a door… | what it buys | what it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **from INSIDE, with you behind it** | nobody gets in without shoving hard, and you hear the shove | nothing. Your enemies are outside and already looking for you |
| **from OUTSIDE, to seal something IN** | it cannot get out | **you have told the whole corridor there is something in there** |
| **from OUTSIDE, to protect a CACHE** | almost nothing | **you have advertised the cache.** A closed door is anonymous; a spiked one is a label |

**So: spike a door you are behind. Think twice about spiking one you are not.**
A cache is better hidden behind an ordinary shut door than behind an obviously
secured one.

### The workaround, and it is a gamble not a fix

You cannot oil an away-swinging door's hinges without first opening it far enough
to reach round the leaf. So: **ease it open an inch or two, oil through the gap,
let it soak, then swing it.** The first inch is the whole risk — a dry pin can
shriek on the first movement as readily as on the full swing, and if it does you
have made the noise *and* not yet got through the door.

*(Caught by the player 2026-09-03 at the west door of (16,20). I had offered to
oil the hinges of a door I had myself ruled opens away from the party. It was
an impossible action and the offer should never have been made.)*

## Frontage — how many fit and fight in a corridor

| passage width | abreast |
|---|---|
| **5 ft** | **single file** |
| **10 ft** | **up to 3** |
| **20 ft** | up to 6 |

*source: **DMG p. 97**, THE CAMPAIGN (The First Dungeon Adventure) — verified from
a 200 dpi page render, printed footer reads 97.*

**Minimum width is about 3½ ft per character.** So a 10' x 10' square holds
**three abreast**; more can *stand* in it, but three is the fighting frontage and
the marching frontage both.

**Consequence for this party's marching order:** the standard order is two
abreast, which leaves **a third slot open in every rank.** In a 10-ft corridor a
lantern-bearer can stand *level with* the front rank rather than behind it — which
is worth 10-20 ft of visible corridor and is the difference between seeing one
square through a doorway and seeing three.

## Searching in PARALLEL — trading rolls for time

**Player's ruling 2026-09-14, day 81, and it is a ruling not a rule.** The DMG
prices **a 10-ft section**, not a searcher: *"searching a 10' section for traps
or secret doors: 1 turn each."* Nothing in the book says how many people may work
one section, so nothing forbids splitting them up.

| how the party works | turns for N sections | rolls per section |
|---|---|---|
| **everybody on every section** | **N** | one per searcher — best odds, slowest |
| **split into pairs** | **N / (searchers / 2)** | **two** |
| **one each, fanned out** | **N / searchers** | **one** — fastest, worst odds |

*source: **DM RULING**, not the DMG. The time figure is the book's; dividing it
across bodies is the player's call, made explicitly as a trade.*

**Expedition 7's find is the shape this preserves:** *"Tristle rolled 2 on his elf
2-in-6 active search and Dellie rolled 1 on her 1-in-6, INDEPENDENTLY, on the
same 10-ft section."* Two searchers, one section, two dice. Splitting them just
puts those two dice on different walls.

**The catch, and it must be said BEFORE the dice:** a section searched by one
person got one roll and **that section is now spent for that person.** Fanning
out is not free — it buys hours at the price of a thinner net, and a 1-in-6 net
with one throw per section misses five sixths of what is there.

## Secret doors — the detection chances

> ### ⚠ THIS ROLL IS REPEATABLE. IT IS NOT A ONCE-PER-OBJECT ABILITY.
>
> *"Thus, a secret door is discovered **1 in 6 by any non-elf, 2 in 6 by elven or
> half-elven characters, EACH CHARACTER BEING ALLOWED TO ROLL EACH TURN** in
> checking a 10' x 10' area."* — **DMG p. 97**
>
> **Each turn.** A section that turned up nothing can be searched again, by the
> same character, for the price of another turn. **Searching costs TIME, and
> nothing else.**
>
> **DO NOT CONFUSE THIS WITH THE THIEF'S ONCE-PER-OBJECT ABILITIES.** Open Locks
> and Find/Remove Traps are *"once per lock"* / *"one try only per thief"* and are
> spent until the character gains a level — `rules/thief.md`. **General searching
> for secret doors is neither of those things.**
>
> *(The DM got this backwards on day 81, telling the player that seven wall
> sections which came up empty could not be retried until somebody levelled. The
> player corrected it: "that's not how general searching works, we can keep
> trying it just takes more time." He is right and the DMG says so in the same
> sentence that gives the odds.)*


| | per turn, per 10' x 10' area searched |
|---|---|
| any non-elf | **1 in 6** |
| **elven or half-elven** | **2 in 6** |
| harder doors | the DM may use d8 or d10 instead of d6 |

*source: **DMG p. 97**.*

And the passive one, which matters for **Tristle** every time the party walks a
corridor: *"An elf doesn't detect secret doors 16⅔% of the time by merely passing
them unless he or she is actually concentrating on the act."* — i.e. **1 in 6 in
passing, without searching**, and 2 in 6 when he stops and works at it.

*source: **DMG p. 97**.*

## Breaking a door down is THREE checks, not one

> *"If wooden doors (always metal bound, naturally) are broken down by axes and
> the like, it will take some time — **a full turn is usual** — and require **at
> least 3 checks** to see if nearby and/or wandering monsters are attracted by
> the noise."*

**This overrides the general "one extra check, not one per action" rule** in
CHECKLIST §5 for this specific act. Smashing a door is a turn of hammering and
**three** rolls.

Also from the same paragraph: **most doors are about 8 ft wide, which allows up
to three characters to try it at once**; a door 3 ft or less admits only one.

*source: **DMG p. 97**. Note this does NOT reinstate the PHB open-doors roll for
B1 — B1's own introduction governs its doors (d6, 1-2 locked, else it opens), and
module front matter beats the DMG's general rule of thumb. See `rules/b1-module.md`.*

## TRACKING — it is a RANGER ability, and this party has no ranger

**Looked up 2026-09-06 after the player asked whether 1e has strict tracking
rules. It does, and the DM had been giving the party ranger-grade reads for
free.**

> **3.** *"Tracking is possible both outdoors and underground in dungeons and
> like settings:*
> **a.** *Underground the ranger must have **observed the creature to be tracked
> within 3 turns (30 minutes)** of the commencement of tracking, and the ranger
> must **begin tracking at a place where the creature was observed**:"*

| Creature's action | Chance to track |
|---|---|
| going along normal passage or room | **65%** |
| passes through normal door or uses stairs | 55% |
| goes through a trap door | 45% |
| chimney or concealed door | 35% |
| passes through a **secret door** | **25%** |

> **b.** *Outdoors: base **90%**, **+2%** per creature above 1, **−10% per 24
> hours** elapsed, **−25%** per hour of precipitation.*

*source: **PHB p. 25**, CHARACTER CLASSES (RANGER), 300 dpi page render 2026-09-06.*

### THE TWO THINGS THAT MATTER

1. **Underground, even a ranger cannot follow COLD tracks.** He must have *seen*
   the creature within 30 minutes and must *start where he saw it*. Tracking
   underground is **pursuit**, not detective work.
2. **No other class gets it.** Not thieves. **This party has no ranger and
   therefore cannot track at all.**

### THE INFRAVISION ROUTE IS ALSO CLOSED

> *"Monsters of a very cold or very warm sort (such as a human) can be **tracked
> infravisually by their footprints. Such tracking must occur within 2 rounds of
> their passing**, or the temperature difference where they had trodden will
> dissipate."*
>
> *"**Light sources which give off heat absolutely prevent normal infravision
> from functioning** within their sphere of illumination."*
>
> — **DMG p. 59**, INFRAVISION & ULTRAVISION, 2026-09-06

**Two rounds, and not while the lantern is lit.** Tristle's elf infravision is a
pursuit tool for a fight in the dark, not a way to read a corridor.

### WHERE THE LINE SITS — what the party MAY observe

**Presence and absence of traffic is environment, not tracking**, and B1's own
text invites it: *"in some portions of the complex, a layer of dust lies upon
everything, undisturbed for years"* (module p. 4) — which only means anything if
disturbance elsewhere is visible.

| **Legitimate for anyone with a lamp** | **RANGER ONLY — do not narrate** |
|---|---|
| "the dust here is packed into a path" | how many made it |
| "the dust here is undisturbed wall to wall" | what size, bare or shod |
| "something has been dragged along here" | which direction they were travelling |
| "this sill has not been opened in months" | "and they came back" |
| | "somebody stands at this exact spot, repeatedly" |
