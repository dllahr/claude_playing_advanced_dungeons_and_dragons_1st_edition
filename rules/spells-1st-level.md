### LIGHT BLINDS — DMG p. 45, and the PHB does not say so

**Found 2026-09-06 by the player after the DM had checked only the PHB and told
him the blinding tactic was not in 1e.** It is, and it is in the book that wins:
**DMG over PHB for adjudication.**

> *"**Light:** It should be noted that if this spell is cast upon the visage or
> before the visual organs of a creature, **it will tend to blind it** (rather as
> if a strong light were placed before its eyes), and its attacks and defenses
> will be a **−4 on 'to hit', saving throws, and even armor class.** Note also
> that the spell is **not mobile**, although it can be cast upon a movable or
> mobile object or creature."*
>
> — **DMG p. 45**, SPELL EXPLANATIONS, cleric first level, 300 dpi render 2026-09-06

| | |
|---|---|
| **On a failed save** | **−4 to hit, −4 on saving throws, and 4 WORSE armour class** (AC 5 becomes AC 9), for the full duration |
| **On a successful save** | PHB p. 44: the globe *"affects the area immediately behind the creature, rather than the creature itself"* — so it still lights the thing up from behind. **A save is not a wasted spell** |
| **Duration at 1st level** | **7 turns** |
| **Mobility** | The globe does **not** move — unless cast on a mobile object or creature, in which case it goes where they go |

**This makes *light* a first-level attack spell, not a lamp.** Seven turns of −4
on everything is better than most things a 1st-level party can do to a single
opponent, and it costs no material component.

*source: **PHB p. 44** for the spell, **DMG p. 45** for the blinding. Both read
from page renders 2026-09-06. The cache carried only the PHB half for weeks.*

## ⚠ IDENTIFY, IN FULL — PHB p. 66-67, and the one-liner was hiding most of it

**Transcribed from a 200 dpi render 2026-09-15, the day before it was to be cast.**
The summary row in the table above says only *"costs 8 points of Constitution, 6
turns rest per point, needs a 100 gp pearl"* — **every one of the following is in
the entry and none of it was cached.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chance per segment** | **15% + 5% per level** that ONE property becomes known. **A 1st-level M-U: 20%** |
| **Duration** | **1 segment PER LEVEL.** A 1st-level M-U gets **ONE segment — one 20% roll, and that is the whole spell** |
| **Then a save** | *"the referee will secretly roll to see if the magic-user made his saving throw versus magic. If the save was successful, the property is known; **if it is 1 point short, A FALSE POWER WILL BE REVEALED**; and if it is lower than 1 under the required score no information will be gained"* |
| **⚠ THE TIME LIMIT** | *"The item to be identified must be examined by the magic-user **WITHIN 1 HOUR PER LEVEL OF EXPERIENCE of the examiner AFTER IT HAS BEEN DISCOVERED**, or all readable impressions will have been blended into those of the characters who have possessed it since"* |
| **⚠ THE COMPONENTS** | a pearl of at least 100 gp — **AND an owl feather steeped in wine, THE INFUSION DRUNK — AND A LIVE MINIATURE CARP SWALLOWED WHOLE**, all prior to casting |
| **The CON cost** | 8 points, **6 turns per point to regain = 48 turns = EIGHT HOURS** |
| **And the floor** | *"If the 8 point loss drops the spell caster below a constitution of 3, he or she will fall unconscious, and consciousness will not be regained until full constitution is restored **24 hours later**"* |
| **A luckstone** | powdered into the infusion: **+25% probability and all saves at +4** |

*source: **PHB p. 66-67**, read from a 200 dpi page render 2026-09-15.*

**WHAT THIS MEANS FOR FRYTHON AND THE RING, and it is not good:**

- **He is 1st level. ONE segment, ONE 20% roll**, then a save that can return a
  **false** power if it misses by exactly 1.
- **CON 12 − 8 = 4.** Above the floor of 3, so he stays conscious — **but only
  just**, and a caster with CON 10 would be out for 24 hours.
- **EIGHT HOURS of rest**, not one. Six turns is an hour; it is **six turns PER
  POINT.**
- **The ring was taken out of the area 27 dais and has been carried for weeks.**
  One hour per level means **one hour**. That window shut long ago.

> ### ⛔ PLAYER'S RULING 2026-09-15: THE STRICT READING. THE WINDOW IS SHUT.
>
> **`identify` can never read the area 27 ring, at any level, ever.** The limit
> runs from **DISCOVERY**, not from the casting — so growing in level lengthens
> the window into a past that has already gone by. A 20th-level magic-user would
> have had twenty hours from the moment it came out of the recess.
>
> **The remaining routes to naming that ring are therefore: a sage; a higher
> divination such as *legend lore*; or PUTTING IT ON.**
>
> **And Frython knew before he left the desk.** He read the entry in order to
> copy it — the limitation is in the same paragraph as the casting time — so the
> party learned this at Wraye and not by wasting a pearl.

## Reversible spells — a MAGIC-USER knows both halves

**DM RULING 2026-09-15, and it is a ruling: NEITHER BOOK STATES A GENERAL RULE
FOR MAGIC-USER REVERSIBLES.** Both were searched.

What the PHB does say, at p. 65: *"**Enlarge** (Alteration) **Reversible.**
Level: I"* — **one entry, one level, one casting time, one set of components** —
and then, inside that same entry, *"**The reverse spell, reduce,** will negate
the effects or actually make creatures or objects smaller in the same ratios."*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Knowing the spell** | **ONE check to know, at the entry's name.** `enlarge` is checked; `reduce` is not a separate spell to check |
| **Memorising it** | **either half, chosen at study**, into the one slot |
| **The book** | holds the entry, which contains both |

*source: **DM RULING 2026-09-15**, not a book rule. PHB p. 65 for the enlarge
entry and its reverse; the absence of a general statement was verified by
searching both the PHB and the DMG.*

**Why:** the reversal is described *within* the entry and is not listed separately
in the PHB's spell list. Requiring a second 75% check would be checking against a
spell the book never names as a spell. *(Asked by the player the moment enlarge
came up at Wraye: "that means he can choose to memorize it any subsequent time as
enlarge or shrink?")*

**1st-level M-U reversibles this matters for:** **enlarge/reduce** ·
**comprehend languages/confuse languages** · **light/darkness** ·
**protection from evil/protection from good**. **Erase has no reverse**; nor do
sleep, identify, shocking grasp, read magic, charm person, dancing lights or
Nystul's magic aura.

### WHAT TO DO WITH SLEEPERS — PHB p. 68, the same entry

> *"The area of effect is determined by the range and **area center decided upon
> by the spell caster**. **Slapping or wounding will awaken affected creatures**,
> but noise will not do so. **Awakening requires 1 complete melee round.** Note
> that **sleeping creatures can be slain automatically at a rate of 1 per slayer
> per melee round**."*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Attacking a sleeper to kill — melee or missile** | **automatic slaying, 1 per slayer per melee round.** No roll, no damage dice, no weapon restriction |
| **Slapping or otherwise deliberately just wounding a sleeper** | wakes it, up one full round later |
| **Noise** | does not wake them |
| **The centre is the caster's choice** | so a 30-ft circle can be placed to catch a doorway and stop short of your own line |

**RULING, day 139, the player's word, corrects an earlier over-reading of this file.** The
printed text draws its line between *"slapping or wounding"* (an incidental or deliberately
non-lethal touch) and *"slain… automatically"* — it does **not** say the automatic slaying is
melee-only, and this file previously said it was. That was this DM's own inference, not the
book's. **An attack made WITH LETHAL INTENT that connects is a slaying**, whatever the
weapon — a bow shot meant to kill a sleeping creature kills it outright, exactly as a sword
stroke would, and is exempt from the normal to-hit/damage rolls the same way melee is.
**Only a strike explicitly meant NOT to kill** (a slap, a called non-lethal blow) risks merely
wounding and waking it instead. So: **fire at will on a sleeping enemy.** The earlier advice
below the old table (do not shoot them) is retracted along with the table it sat under.

*source: **PHB p. 68**, SLEEP, text layer read 2026-09-06 and the rate confirmed
word for word. The campaign had been citing "1 per slayer per round, PHB p.68"
from expedition 6 onward; that citation is exactly right — the melee-only gloss added on top
of it, on day 139, was not.*

### SLEEP DOES NOT DISTINGUISH FRIEND FROM FOE

**Read the PHB's own words, verified from the page 2026-09-04:**

> *"When a magic-user casts a sleep spell, he or she will usually cause a
> comatose slumber to come upon **one or more creatures** [other than undead and
> certain other creatures specifically excluded]. **All creatures to be affected
> by the sleep spell must be within a 3" diameter circle.**"*
>
> *"**The area of effect is determined by the range and area center decided upon
> by the spell caster.**"*

**"Creatures." Not "enemies."** There is no friend-or-foe clause anywhere in the
entry, and the spell has **no saving throw**. The caster chooses the *centre*,
and everything in the circle is a candidate.

**So the geometry is the whole tactical question.** A 3" circle is **30 ft
across — 15 ft in every direction from the centre.** To catch an enemy in
CONTACT with your own front rank, the circle must contain your front rank. There
is no way to draw it otherwise.

**Which means sleep is a spell for enemies at a distance, not for a melee you
are already in.** Frython's one previous casting was textbook: a lone kobold at
forty feet, nobody else within a hundred.

### WHO is affected when the circle holds more than 4d4 covers — the DMG answers it

> ***"Sleep: Unless a single creature is designated as the target of a sleep
> spell in a mixed group, the sleep spell will first affect the lowest
> level/hit dice targets."***
>
> — **DMG p. 45**, SPELL EXPLANATIONS (MAGIC-USER SPELLS), verified from the
> page; printed footer reads 45.

**Two rules in one sentence, and the first is easy to miss:**

1. **The caster MAY designate a single creature as the target.** One named
   victim, and the spell does not spread. Costly — it throws away 4d4 creatures'
   worth — but it is the way to sleep one thing standing next to your friends.
2. **Otherwise it takes the LOWEST LEVEL / HIT DICE first**, friend or foe.

**THE TIE PROBLEM, and it is this party's whole situation.** At 1st level
*everyone* is level 1 / 1 HD, and so are orcs, kobolds and goblins. "Lowest
first" then orders nothing. **The DMG does not break the tie.**

**Ruling for this campaign: when the circle holds more 1-HD creatures than 4d4
covers, roll for which are affected — randomly, in the open, no favouring
either side.** Recorded 2026-09-04 so it is not decided in the caster's favour
in the moment.

**And the sting in the tail, same entry:** *"Note that sleeping creatures can be
slain automatically."* That cuts both ways and it is why this matters.

**The two limits that decide whether it wins a fight:** the **3" circle**, so a
strung-out enemy cannot all be caught; and **five rounds**, which is long enough
to bind a prisoner and not much else. It is not a sleep that lasts the night.

## Recovery of spells — minimum rest (DMG p. 40)

**Rest is set by the HIGHEST spell level to be recovered**, not by how many spells
are wanted. A caster who wants six 2nd-level spells rests 4 hours; one who wants a
single 7th rests 10.

| Spell level | 1-2 | 3-4 | 5-6 | 7-8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rest time | **4 hours** | 6 hours | 8 hours | 10 hours | 12 hours |

*source: **DMG p. 40**, RECOVERY OF SPELLS, 200 dpi page render 2026-08-29.*

> *"Whether one or more spells are to be regained, the minimum time required for
> complete rest (usually sleep) is that required for the highest spell to be
> recovered… Once rested, an additional one-quarter hour per level of spell must
> be spent in study/prayer and meditation in order to memorize/acquire the ability
> to cast each spell."* — DMG p. 40

**Two separate costs, and the second is per spell:**

1. **Rest** (usually sleep) — 4 hours for anything 1st or 2nd level. Paid once.
2. **Study / prayer** — **¼ hour per spell level, for EACH spell**. A 1st-level
   spell costs 15 minutes. Paid per spell, after the rest.

Everyone here casts 1st-level spells only, so the rest is **4 hours** for all of
them and the memorisation is **15 minutes a spell**:

| Caster | Slots | Study time for a full complement |
|---|---|---|
| Frython (M-U 1, INT 17) | 1 | 15 minutes |
| Yrtone (Cleric 1, WIS 16) | 3 | 45 minutes |
| Blartho (Cleric 1, WIS 15) | 3 | 45 minutes |

*source: slot counts from `campaign/spells.tsv`; times computed from DMG p. 40 above.*

**A caster who still holds an unspent spell loses nothing by re-memorising**, but

indefinitely and needs no rest at all — Frython's *shocking grasp* has ridden
along unspent since round 1460.
