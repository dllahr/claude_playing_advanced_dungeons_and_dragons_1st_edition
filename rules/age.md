# Age and aging

**Rolled for the whole party on day 99, 2026-09-20, at the player's request** —
prompted by his catching the DM write that the library's thirty-year-old papers
were *"older than Tristle."* **Tristle is an elf.** The papers are younger than
he is by ninety years.

Age in 1e is **DM-side**: both tables are in the DMG, not the PHB.

*"Bards begin at the age of the class in which they first begin."*

**Fighters start YOUNGEST of all the human classes — 16 to 19.** That is the
book, not a quirk of this campaign's dice, and it is worth knowing before being
surprised by it.

> *"At the onset of each and every character's creation it is necessary that you
> establish his or her age. **For player characters and henchmen you must use the
> appropriate table.** You may do the same for other characters, or **you may
> assign age as you see fit in light of the milieu you have developed**."*

That last clause is what lets an NPC's established age stand against the table —
see *Hask and Brand* below.

## The ability adjustments — and WHEN they apply

| Category | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| **Young Adult** | −1 wisdom, +1 constitution |
| **Mature** | +1 strength, +1 wisdom |
| **Middle Aged** | −1 strength (or half exceptional), −1 constitution; +1 intelligence, +1 wisdom |
| **Old** | −2 strength, −2 dexterity, −1 constitution; +1 wisdom |
| **Venerable** | −1 strength, −1 dexterity, −1 constitution; +1 intelligence, +1 wisdom |

*source: **DMG p. 13**, AGING, the five category paragraphs, same render.*

> *"When age category is established, modify ability scores accordingly, **making
> each change progressively from young adulthood, all additions and subtractions
> being cumulative**… The only ability which may exceed 18 due to age effects
> (unless age restricts this) is **wisdom**. Most adjustments are in whole
> numbers, so that 18 strength drops to 17, even if it is from 18/00, as
> exceptional strength is not considered."*

> *"Adjustments cannot exceed racial maximums nor can they be used if they cause
> abilities to exceed stated maximums. Likewise, any adjustments cannot lower any
> ability below racial or class minimums."*

> ### ⚠ RULING: THE ADJUSTMENTS ARE NOT APPLIED RETROACTIVELY. Day 99.
>
> **The scores as rolled and played ARE each character's scores at their current
> age.** The bracket adjustments fire when a character **CROSSES** a bracket —
> not as a recalculation of a character already in one.
>
> **Why**, in order of weight:
>
> 1. **This campaign's standing law: a rules correction is prospective, never
>    retroactive.** Applying brackets to nine characters on day 99 would change
>    Strength, Constitution and Wisdom — and therefore hit points, to-hit, and
>    every `hp_log.tsv` row computed against a Constitution that was different
>    at the time. **Ninety-nine days of resolved play would be wrong**, and the
>    logs are append-only by design.
> 2. **The text describes a progression, not a recalculation**: *"making each
>    change progressively from young adulthood."* It is written for tracking a
>    character through time, which is what the surrounding section is about —
>    *"Once character age is established, you must keep track of it from game
>    year to game year."*
> 3. Nothing in the generation methods this campaign used — PHB Method I for the
>    six PCs, DMG p.11 for the henchmen — mentions an age pass afterwards.
>
> **SO THE RULE STAYS LIVE AND MATTERS GOING FORWARD**, which is the point of
> writing it down rather than waving it off. 1e ages characters unnaturally and
> often:
>
> | cause | years |
> |---|---|
> | casting *gate* | 5 |
> | casting *alter reality*, *resurrection*, *wish* | 3 each |
> | casting *restoration* | 2 |
> | casting *limited wish* | 1 |
> | **imbibing a *speed* potion** | **1** |
> | **under a *haste* spell** | **1** |
>
> *"Reading one of the above spells from a scroll (or using the power from a ring
> or other device) does not cause unnatural aging, but **placing such a spell
> upon the scroll in the first place will do so**!"*
>
> **Hask and Brand are both 41 — one year inside Middle Aged.** They are the two
> who will cross first, and a haste spell or a ghost would do it. When one does
> cross, the adjustment applies **then**, is logged as an event with a day, and
> is narrated as conduct, not as statistics.
>
> *source for the aging causes: **DMG p. 13**, Unnatural Aging.*

39, at 41, and Brand has no class at all.

**`campaign/characters.tsv` holds the numbers and is authoritative.** They are
ages at creation, and the campaign is 99 days old, so they are also the current
ages; `campaign/days.tsv` is the authority on elapsed time.
