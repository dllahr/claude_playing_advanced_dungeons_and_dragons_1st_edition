## Cost of living in town — HOUSE RULE

**The DMG's monthly upkeep is NOT used in this campaign.** The party pays for
what it uses: **1 gp per person per night** for lodging and board in town.

| | Cost |
|---|---|
| One character, one night | **1 gp** |
| Six characters, one night | 6 gp |
| Six characters, a seven-night stay | **42 gp** |

*source: house rule, confirmed by the player 2026-08-25. It is what has been
charged since expedition 1.*

### The rule being set aside, and why

> *"Each player character will automatically expend not less than 100 gold
> pieces per level of experience per month. This is simply support, upkeep,
> equipment, and entertainment expense. These costs are to be deducted by the
> Dungeon Master automatically."*
>
> — **DMG p. 25**, PLAYER CHARACTER EXPENSES, read from the text layer 2026-08-25.

At 100 gp per level per month, six 1st-level characters owe 150 gp for a week.
The player's objection, and it is correct on the facts: **the party never had
that kind of money.** Starting funds were 70–150 gp each, and the whole purse
after three expeditions is under 500 gp. Charged as written, upkeep alone would
consume most of every haul before a single flask of oil was bought, which is
not how this campaign has been run since expedition 1.

Applied retroactively 2026-08-25: a 150 gp deduction was reversed and replaced
with 42 gp for the seven-night stay before expedition 4.

**Selling salvage.** No 1e table sets a resale price for used arms; the
campaign has settled on **half the PHB list price**, which is what expedition
1's salvage fetched (9 gp 8 sp). Recorded here so the same number is used every
time rather than re-invented.

| **limner — 10 s.p./day** | *"all sign painting, drawing of heraldic devices"* |

**SO: A MESSENGER IS 3 s.p. A DAY.** *(First needed day 41, for Wat's ride to Ede
and Lyde.)*

**And note what a TEAMSTER is, because it bears on Halm.** A teamster at 5 s.p./day
is a man hired to drive somebody else's cart. **Halm owns his cart**, which makes
him not a hireling but a business — and it is why *"one load's a load; what I'd
want is a run"* is a different ask from a wage.

> *"**Location Of Standard Hirelings:** In general the various occupations
> represented here are common to most settlements of **village-size and above**,
> although each and every village will not be likely to furnish each and every
> sort of common hireling. Towns and cities will have many available."*
> — DMG p. 28

*source: **DMG p. 28**, the occupation notes beneath the STANDARD HIRELINGS
TABLE, 300 dpi page render 2026-09-06.*

**That is the answer to an encumbrance problem, and it is almost free** — a
shilling a day. Two porters with a carrying pole move **200 lb** that nobody in
the party has to carry.

### THE CATCH, AND IT IS THE WHOLE CATCH

**These rates are for ordinary labour, not for going down a hole full of
monsters.** A porter at 1 s.p./day is a man who carries sacks. Nothing on this
table is a mercenary — the DMG puts **men-at-arms under EXPERT HIRELINGS**, a
different section and a different price, precisely because fighting is a
different job.

Taking a standard hireling into Quasqueton means:

- a **wage that reflects the risk**, negotiated, not table-priced
- **morale**, which laborers have in short supply the first time something screams
- responsibility for a civilian the party brought into danger

**Cheap porters are for the road and the entrance. Anything past the door is a
negotiation.**

## Hiring an animal — DM call, no book price exists

Neither the PHB nor the DMG prices the **hire** of a horse or mule; both price
buying it outright and both price hiring *people* (DMG p.28, above).

**Ruling: 3 s.p. a day for a mule or draft horse, plus a deposit against its
loss.** Anchored on the DMG's own hireling scale — a bearer is 1 s.p. a day, and
an animal that carries four times as much and represents 20-30 gp of somebody's
capital is worth about three times a man's day. **Set 2026-08-30; keep it.**

**The deposit is the real term**, not the fee. Nobody lets 30 gp of working
animal walk out of town on a handshake.

## Pack animal carrying capacity — horses and the mule

Loads are in **gold pieces of weight**; 10 gp weight = 1 pound (PHB p. 101). The
first figure is the most the animal carries at its **normal** move, the second
the most it will carry **at all**, at half that move.

| Animal | Move | Max load, normal move | Max load, half move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mule** | **12"** | **2,000** | **6,000** |
| Draft horse | 12" | 4,000 | 8,000 |
| Heavy warhorse | 15" | 5,000 | 7,500 |
| Medium warhorse | 18" | 4,000 | 6,500 |
| Light warhorse | 24" | 3,000 | 5,000 |
| Pony | 12" | 2,000 | 3,000 |
| Wild horse | 24" | 3,000 | 6,000 |

*source: **MM p. 53**, HORSE, and **MM p. 72**, MULE, 200 dpi page renders
2026-08-31. Moves are from the stat blocks on the same pages.*

> *"The various types of horses can carry the following maximum loads, indicated
> in gold pieces of weight. The number before the slash is normal movement, the
> number after is encumbered movement maximum load. Encumbrance slows speed
> one-half."* — **MM p. 53**
>
> *"A mule can carry 2000 gold pieces in weight at normal speed, 6000 at one-half
> speed."* — **MM p. 72**

**There is no third band and no proportional scale.** A load is either inside the
normal figure, or it is between that and the maximum and the animal moves at half
speed, or it is over the maximum and the animal does not carry it. The books give
no penalty between the two — 2,001 gp weight on a mule costs the same half speed
as 5,999 does.

**Why the mule and not the horse.** MM p. 53 ends the horse entry: *"Horses (and
ponies) are not sufficiently agile to take into dungeons."* MM p. 72 says of the
mule: *"strong and agile, thus able to negotiate dungeons well in most cases."*
The draft horse carries twice the mule's load and is only 10 gp dearer; the mule
is the adventuring animal because of that one sentence, not because of its back.
The mule entry also warns it is *"likely to bite or kick their own handler in a
contrary mood"*, is **not** panicked by fire (horses are, 90% of the time), but
may bolt or **bray loudly** at strange smells.

## Animals the books give NO load for — DM call

**The PHB and DMG contain no carrying capacity for any animal at all.** PHB
encumbrance is pp. 101-102 and covers only characters; the DMG index has no entry
for horses, pack animals or beasts of burden, and its encumbrance entry points
only to Appendix O's item list. Every load figure above comes from the Monster
Manual, and the Monster Manual has **no donkey entry and no ox entry** — neither
does Monster Manual II. The PHB prices both (donkey 8 gp, ox 15 gp, p. 35) and
then never says what they can carry.

The DMG's only mention of them is occupational: a **pack handler** is *"trained at
loading, handling, and unloading beasts of burden such as donkeys, mules, horses,
etc."* and a **teamster** is an expert *"with respect to their particular
specialty of draft animal only, i.e. horses, mules, oxen, or whatever"* (DMG
p. 28). That is a job description, not a load table.

| Animal | Normal move | Max load, normal move | Max load, half move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Riding horse (light) | 24" | 3,000 | 5,000 |
| Donkey | 12" | 1,000 | 3,000 |
| Ox, packed | 9" | 2,000 | 6,000 |
| Ox, in draft (cart or plough) | 9" | 8,000 | — |

*source: **DM call, set 2026-08-31 — these are NOT book figures.** No 1e core book
gives a load for a donkey, an ox, or a riding horse. Anchors below.*

- **Riding horse:** the PHB prices *Horse, riding (light)* at 25 gp (p. 35); the
  MM's "Light" column is the light **war**horse, a 150 gp animal. Same beast,
  untrained for war, so its load is taken unchanged from MM p. 53.
- **Donkey:** the mule is *"a sterile hybrid between horses and donkeys"* (MM
  p. 72) and the smaller parent. Priced at 8 gp against the mule's 20. Ruled at
  half a mule.
- **Ox:** a draft animal, not a pack animal — the DMG lists it under the teamster,
  who drives carts. The packed figure is a mule's; the draft figure is the draft
  horse's normal-move load (MM p. 53), on the reasoning that an ox out-pulls a
  horse and under-walks it. **If an ox is being ridden or packed, something has
  gone wrong.**

> **⚠ BUT THE TRAIL ONLY GOES HALFWAY.** `campaign/calendar.md`: the basecamp *"sits
> HALFWAY between the road and the cave mouth"* — **road → basecamp is graded and
> cart-passable; BASECAMP → CAVE ENTRANCE is another half hour of unmade outcrop.**
> **A cart never reaches the door.** The 460 lb has to come down that half hour by
> hand, rope or drag. *(DM error 2026-09-08, player-caught: Halm was written

>
> **NO BOOK GIVES A CART'S CAPACITY.** PHB p.36 prices a cart at 50 gp and says
> nothing about load. **DM CALL, recorded so it is the same number every time: a
> beast in a cart shifts about FOUR TIMES what it carries on its back**, which is
> the whole reason wheels exist. So **one mule and a cart ≈ 8,000 gp weight at
> normal move** — the barrel and the glass and the jars together, comfortably.
> *(Reasoning, not a book figure: rolling resistance against dead weight. If a
> better source turns up it wins.)*
>
> **AND THE REAL CONSTRAINT IS NOT THE CART.** Halm's own two questions, day 66:
> **getting 460 lb out of the dungeon**, which no cart helps with; and whether the
> contents are worth the cart-space, since ~97 gp of glass and forty jars are
> waiting in area 8. **The keg, spigot and gimlet bought day 34 for ~2 gp still
> answer the second one: tap it up there and decant what is worth carrying.** At walking pace it
is not one or the other — it is *neither*, and the biggest cask the mule takes at
normal speed alongside the empty chest is a **firkin** (1,150 + 250 = 1,400,
inside 1,450 by 50).

**At half speed (6"), payload 5,450:**

| | gp weight |
|---|---|
| Small wooden chest, empty | 100-250 |
| Barrel of ale, 36 gallon | 4,600 |
| **total** | **4,700-4,850** |
| against a half-speed payload of | 5,450 |
| **slack left for whatever is in the chest** | **600-750** |

*source: chest **DMG p. 225** (Appendix O); mule load **MM p. 72**; the barrel
weight and the 550 gp weight of rig are the **DM calls** above. Arithmetic only.*

**So: yes, one mule does it in one trip — at half speed and with an all but empty
chest.** 600 gp weight of slack is 600 coins, or one more small item; a small
chest packed with coin (a thousand coins is 1,000 gp weight on its own) puts the
load at 5,850-6,000 and then over the mule's absolute maximum, and it becomes two
trips or two animals.

**What half speed costs on the hillside.** PHB p. 102: outdoors, *"Each 1" equals
the number of miles a character or creature can travel in one-half day's
trekking."* A mule at 12" makes 12 miles in a half day; at 6" it makes six — call
it **about a mile an hour** over a six-hour half-day, before any terrain
adjustment. **DM call: rough hillside, descending, with a mule loaded to within
600 gp weight of the maximum it can physically carry — half that again, and roll
for the load shifting.** An hour of it covers perhaps half a mile.

**The short version, book figures separated from judgement:**

- **Book:** a mule carries **2,000 gp weight at 12", 6,000 at 6"** (MM p. 72). A
  small wooden chest is **100-250 gp weight** (DMG p. 225).
- **Book:** nothing about tack changes those numbers.
- **DM call:** the rig costs 550 gp weight; a 36-gallon barrel of ale is 4,600.
- **Answer:** at walking pace, neither. At half pace, both — barely, and only if
  the chest is nearly empty. **Two mules and it is a walk.**

## Duties, fees and taxes on a trading town (DMG p. 90)

Gygax's own worked example, adopted as this campaign's system because the books
give no other. **It is an example, not universal law** — a different town may
differ, and that is the DM's call, but this is the default.

| charge | citizen | **foreigner** |
|---|---|---|
| **Duty** on normal goods brought in for sale — foodstuffs, **cloth and hides**, livestock, **raw materials**, manufactured goods | 1% | **2%** |
| **Tariff**, *in addition to duty*, on luxury and precious goods — wine, spirits, furs, metals, jewelry | **5% of value if they are to be sold** | 5% |
| **Entry fee**, per head (man **or animal**) or **per wheel** | 1 cp | **5 cp** |
| **Sales tax** | — | **10% on all foreigners** ("although no service tax is levied upon them") |
| Head tax, annual | 1 cp peasant / 1 sp freeman / 1 gp gentleman | foreign residents are stopped and asked for proof |
| **Tolls** | on the main route, *"according to the numbers of persons, animals, carts, wagons, and possibly even materials transported"* | |

*source: **DMG p. 90**, DUTIES, EXCISES, FEES, TARIFFS, TAXES, TITHES AND TOLLS,
text layer read 2026-09-01 and cross-checked against the surrounding block.*

### The three that bite

- **"Special forms for sale are then given to the person so declaring his wares
  (OTHERWISE NO LEGAL SALE IS POSSIBLE)."** Declared luxury goods come with
  paperwork, and a seller without it cannot legally sell.
- **"Unless they have official passports to allow free entry."** A lord's passport
  waives the entry fee. **Sir Aymer could write one.**
- **An excise may be levied on foreign currency** *"in order to change it into the
  less remarkable coin of the realm."* Coin brought from elsewhere may cost money
  simply to spend.

### WHEN each is paid — the practical question

| | paid |
|---|---|
| Tolls | **on the road**, as you reach them |
| Entry fee | **at the gate**, before anything |
| **Duty** | **at the gate, on the assessed value of goods brought in FOR SALE** — before you have sold anything or know what you will get |
| **Sales tax** | **on the sale**, so it comes out of the proceeds |

**So a trading party needs cash in hand at the gate**, before a penny comes in.
For the Ludbury run: ~2% of the wool and the rosewood ≈ **32 gp**, plus entry at
5 cp a head for **thirteen people, four animals and six wheels** ≈ **1 gp 2 sp**,
plus tolls. **The 10% sales tax — ~130 gp on a 1,300 gp sale — comes off the
back end.**

**The duty is assessed on the TOWN'S valuation, not the seller's**, and the
assessor's interest runs the other way from yours.


## Holy water — WHO CAN MAKE IT AND WHAT IT TAKES (DMG pp. 114-115)

**The 25 gp on the Religious items list above is a CITY price for a finished
vial.** It is not a thing a country chapel can sell you, and the reason is here.

> *"Only clerics, excluding druids, are able to prepare holy water — or unholy
> water in the case of evil clerics. **As a third level spell is involved, it
> requires a cleric of not less than 5th level of experience** to manage to create
> such fluid."*

**Three hard gates, all of them.**

| gate | requirement |
|---|---|
| **THE PRIEST** | a cleric of **5th level or better**. Not 4th. Druids never |
| **THE FONT** | a blessed basin of **precious metal** (copper, silver, electrum, gold or platinum), engraved with the holy symbols, set inside a **font** of finely worked rare wood with base, pedestal, chest-like holder and lid. **Made to special order, 2d4+2 = 4-10 weeks.** **ONE FONT PER RELIGIOUS EDIFICE** — deities *"look upon anything other as unworthy and excessive"* |
| **THE VIALS** | once made, holy water **cannot leave the font for more than a turn without losing its efficacy** unless it is in specially **blessed vials of crystal — rock crystal or special leaded glass.** Each empty vial is worth **2-5 gp** |

*source: **DMG pp. 114-115**, same section as the receptacle table below.*

**The ritual**, robed in formal vestments, casts in succession: **create water ·
purify food & drink · bless · chant (1 full turn) · prayer.**

**LIMIT: once per week and no more** — a full day of prayers and meditation for
the ritual, then **not less than 8 hours of rest** after it.

### Holy/unholy water receptacles

| Metal | Capacity for creation | Basin cost | Font cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| copper | **6 vials** | 130 - 180 gp | **200 gp** |
| silver | 10 vials | 1,900 - 2,400 gp | 500 gp |
| electrum | 18 vials | 8,000 - 12,000 gp | 1,000 gp |
| gold | 32 vials | 19,000 - 22,000 gp | 1,500 gp |
| platinum | 50 vials | 110,000 - 200,000 gp | 2,000 gp |

*source: **DMG pp. 114-115**, "MAGICAL RESEARCH — CREATION OF HOLY/UNHOLY WATER", 150 dpi page renders 2026-09-07.*

**Combination vessels are allowed** by interpolation — a copper basin chased with
silver and set with a silver rim and handles holds 8 vials and costs 50% of the
copper price plus 50% of the silver price.

**Capacity is deliberately a supply limit**, and the book says so: *"Capacities
are designed for game purposes so as to limit supplies of holy/unholy water."*

### Two more clauses worth knowing

- **DEFILEMENT.** If a non-believer blesses/curses the opposite kind of font, or
  uses *"less refined means such as excreting wastes into a font or basin"*, the
  whole thing is **desecrated and unfit and must be smelted down and remade**, at
  20-50% of the costs above and 4-6 weeks. **Requires actual contact** — blessing
  or cursing at a distance is wasted.
- **DRINKING IT.** Ingesting or bathing with holy water **slows the onset of
  lycanthropy or of becoming undead by 1-4 turns per vial**, secretly determined.

### What this means for THIS party — day 51

**Nobody the party can reach can make holy water.**

- **Yrtone and Blartho are 1st level.** They are four levels short.
- **Father Cuddy is 3rd** (rolled day 51, d6=3). He is two levels short and says
  so himself.
- **His chapel has no font** (d6=5) and never has — but it is a consecrated
  edifice in its own right, so it is **eligible** for one.
- **The cheapest working arrangement in the book is copper: 130-180 for the basin
  + 200 for the font = 330-380 gp, 4-10 weeks to build, six vials a week
  thereafter.** The party can afford that today.
- **And it would be worth nothing without a 5th-level cleric to use it.** That is
  the ordering problem: **the font is the cheap half.**
