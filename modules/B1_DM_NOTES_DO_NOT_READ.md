## AD&D 1e conversion notes

The module's stat blocks are **Basic D&D**. Running 1e, so at point of use:

- **Movement**: `90'(30')` → `9"`, `120'(40')` → `12"`, `60'(20')` → `6"`, `30'(10')` → `3"`.
- **Carrion crawler (r5) is materially nastier in 1e.** MM gives AC **3/7** (head/body),
  MV 12", HD 3+1, **8 tentacle attacks**, paralysis on failed save. The module lists a flat
  AC 7 and 6 hp. Use MM stats — this is the single most dangerous placed encounter and can
  wipe a 1st-level party. It is in Zelligar's chamber, behind a secret door, so a cautious
  party need not meet it.
- **Ghouls (r22)**: 1e paralysis does **not** affect elves. Relevant if an elf is rolled.
- **Morale**: module gives Basic ML 2–12. Use DMG p. 67 percentage morale instead.
- **XP**: award by DMG Appendix E (base + hp bonus), not Basic values.
- **Saves**: monsters save as fighters of their HD.

## Round 1668 — THE POLE TRIPPED THE ILLUSION. Dave's plan, and the ruling behind it

treasure **through** the pole, and there is no reading of a permanent illusion in

# RULINGS — day 39, 2026-09-05: the three henchmen become characters

**All of these are DM rulings on questions the three core books do not answer.**
The research and every page citation are in `rules/zero-level.md`; what follows
is only what was *decided*, so a later session does not silently decide it
differently.

**The gap being filled.** 1e has no 0-level → 1st-level progression. 1st level
*begins* at 0 XP (PHB pp. 20, 22), so there is no threshold to cross; the gate is
DMG p. 35's "1 in 100 suitable for level advancement", which is a capacity, not a
score. The books describe no transition, and searching all three for one returns
nothing.

| # | ruled | reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **They are among the advancement-capable. No die rolled.** | DMG p. 35 derives its 1-in-1,000 henchman pool *from* the 1-in-100 capable pool — in the book's own model, taking service with an adventurer is the evidence of capacity. Rolling to see whether the person who did the thing is the kind of person who does the thing is backwards. **If we RECRUIT someone new, roll the 1 in 100 then** — that is the case the ratio is written for. |
| **2** | **1st level immediately. No waiting period, no XP threshold.** | There is nothing to wait for. Inventing one would be a 5e-shaped intuition with no 1e text behind it. |
| **3** | **Class: ability scores gate, then background picks among the legal options.** | DMG p. 35's d100 class roll is written for a faceless applicant pool. These three have forty sessions of history. Scores were rolled first and in the open so the gate was honest. |
| **4** | **Scores by DMG p. 11 "Special Characters, Including Henchmen"** — 3d6 full range, germane ability boosted (+1 to each die under 6) **after** the class was fixed. | The alternative, "General Characters", forces every die to 3 or 4 and yields nothing but 9-12 across the board — no prime-requisite bonus possible, ever. |
| **5** | **Hit points: keep the higher of current hp and a fresh class die.** | Silence in the books. Losing hp for advancing would be perverse; the only analogous printed mechanic (DMG p. 119 energy drain) *removes* hp for losing a level. **Moot in the event — all three rolled exactly what they already had.** |
| **6** | **MONSTER xp is now a FULL share. TREASURE xp stays HALF.** | DMG p. 85's halving sentence sits inside "Division of Experience Points", which opens on *slain monsters* and is explicitly "strictly your prerogative". The next heading, EXPERIENCE VALUE OF TREASURE TAKEN, never mentions henchmen — treasure xp is 1 gp = 1 xp on coin gained, so a half share of coin already halves it. **Held prospective by a day-39 anchor row**; without it the pot divisor going 7 → 8 would have retroactively taken xp off the six PCs. |
| **7** | **They remain HENCHMEN, not PCs.** | Corm and Sarn are not, and should not be swept in. |
| **8** | **Training at 1st→2nd costs them the same as anyone: level × 1,500/week.** | DMG p. 86 says "character" throughout and exempts nobody. Say plainly that the free ride ended. |

## AREAS 15 & 16 — THE PAIRED TELEPORTATION ROOMS. Worked out day 95, BEFORE contact

**The party is at (5,37) with area 15's door in front of them and it came up
LOCKED (d6=1).** So there is time to get this right on paper. **Botching it live
would ruin the single best trick in the module**, and the module's own
instruction — *"the DM must turn his or her map on its side"* — is precisely the
sort of thing that gets fumbled under narrative pressure.

### Geometry, read off the database, not guessed

| | area 15 | area 16 |
|---|---|---|
| squares | (3,37)(3,38)(3,39)(4,37)(4,38)(4,39) | (2,33)(2,34)(3,33)(3,34)(4,33)(4,34) |
| shape | **2 wide × 3 deep**, x=3-4 | **3 wide × 2 deep**, x=2-4 |
| door | **(4,37,V)** — EAST wall, south end | **(2,32,H)** — SOUTH wall, west end |
| outside the door | **(5,37)**, the corridor | **(2,32)**, then (2,31) |
| farthest corner | **(3,39)** | **(4,34)** |

**In door-relative terms both rooms are 2 deep by 3 wide.** The module's *"equal
size and shape"* is exactly true and the apparent difference is only rotation.

### ⛔ THE SQUARE MAPPING — computed, do not re-derive under pressure

| in area 15 | → in area 16 |
|---|---|
| (4,37) door square | (2,33) |
| (4,38) | (3,33) |
| (4,39) | (4,33) |
| (3,37) | (2,34) |
| (3,38) | (3,34) |
| **(3,39) the outcropping** | **(4,34) the outcropping** |

### ⛔ THE DIRECTION TRANSFORM: BELIEVED → ACTUAL IS 90° CLOCKWISE

The door is on the **east** wall in 15 and the **south** wall in 16, and the
party *will go on believing it is east*. So:

| they believe | it is really |
|---|---|
| north | **east** |
| east | **south** |
| south | **west** |
| west | **north** |

**So when they leave "the way they came in" they walk SOUTH out of (2,32)** into
corridor they have never seen, convinced they are heading east back toward the
(5,37) dogleg. *That* is the moment the trick lands — not the teleport itself.

### The trigger, verbatim, and what it catches

> *"Once adventurers **enter the room to investigate this**, **the entire party**
> is instantly teleported to identical locations at the other room — **whether
> they be in the room itself or nearby in the hallway.** This teleportation
> occurs without the adventurer noticing that it has occurred; that is, they have
> **no way of 'feeling'** that anything unusual has happened."*

- **Opening the door does not fire it.** Entering to investigate the outcropping does.
- **THE WHOLE PARTY GOES, INCLUDING ANYONE LEFT ON GUARD IN THE CORRIDOR.** A
  rearguard is no protection and posting one must not be hinted to be.
- **No save, no sensation, no roll.** Do not offer one.
- The outcropping is *"a shiny, sparkling outcropping of crystalline rock which
  will dazzle when light is reflected off of it"* — and **identical in both
  rooms**, which is what makes the second visit indistinguishable from the first.

### How to run it without lying

**Describe what they see, in the directions they believe they are facing.** The
map is turned, not the narration. Never volunteer a bearing they could not have
checked. **If they ask a direct question about direction, answer from their
frame** — they have no compass and no sun; a wrong answer from me would be a lie,
but "the corridor runs the way you remember" is simply their own dead reckoning
and it is what a character would say.

**And when it unravels — and the module predicts the exact words, *"wait a
minute, that can't be like that, we just came that way"* — DO NOT CONFIRM OR
DENY.** Let them test it. The honest tools are all on their side: pace counts,
Tristle's and Ista's counting, the mapped squares behind them, and the fact that
**this party writes everything down.**

### ⚠ AND THE THING THAT WILL ACTUALLY EXPOSE IT

They have a **map database** and they log every square they walk. The moment
they compare a wall they can see against a wall they recorded, the geometry will
not fit. **That is a fair win and must be allowed to happen.** Do not quietly
adjust anything to keep the illusion alive.
